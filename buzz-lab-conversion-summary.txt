- Needs fixed mode: can become numeric auto-graded if a locked preset/Buzz mode is added.
- Already handled: existing question/template work exists or the user said it is done.

Unit 2 Lesson 5 - Inertia and Tension Demo
Summary:
- Level 1 is the cup, index card, and penny demo. During a slow pull, friction carries the penny with the card; during a quick pull, inertia keeps the penny nearly at rest so it drops into the cup.
- Level 2 is the string-tension demo. Students observe a mass hanging between two strings and compare what happens during a slow pull versus a fast pull.
- In Level 2, Slow Pull breaks the top string because the top string must support the 9.8 N weight plus the 15.0 N pull, giving about 24.8 N of top tension.
- In Level 2, Fast Pull breaks the bottom string because inertia keeps the mass from responding immediately, causing the bottom string tension to spike to about 45.0 N.
- Key ideas include inertia, tension, weight, force transfer, and Newton's First Law.
Buzz fit:
- Strong Buzz-native fit. The demo has fixed observable results, and the self-contained template embeds the canvas lab directly above the native Buzz questions with a stage bar to switch between Level 1 and Level 2.

Unit 3 Lesson 4 - Collision Lab: Elastic and Inelastic Collisions
Summary:
- Replaces the PhET Collision Lab worksheet with a hosted 1D collision simulator plus a chart-styled Buzz assessment.
- The simulator (collison-lab.html) is a pure sim with FIXED initial conditions for every trial - no mass or speed controls. Trial tabs: Trial 1 stationary target (100%), Trial 2 head-on (100%), Trial 3 rear-end stick (0%), Trial 4 head-on stick (0%), Explore (elasticity slider 0-100% with computed totals). Each trial's setup shows in a chip bar; readouts sit above the canvas. Students use the fixed trials to prove conservation of momentum and examine when KE is conserved. The learning-goals card, data chart card, lab-evidence card, and inline directions were removed from the sim and live in the Buzz template and the Guide/Hint overlays.
- The Buzz template is HTML/CSS only (no JavaScript) and looks like a lab data chart: a static setup table fills in the recorded/set values, and each chart cell holds a separate <a:question /> placeholder.
- Questions 1-4 are numeric-input questions for the Trial 1 calculated totals (initial/final momentum and KE); Question 5 is the analysis essay.
- Sign convention is a graded skill: the simulator never shows +/- signs. Every velocity/momentum readout is a magnitude with a direction arrow, and students convert arrows to signs (right = +, left = -) before calculating momentum totals.
- Numeric auto-grading works because Trial 1's conditions are fixed: m1=1.0 kg moving right at 3.0 m/s, m2=2.0 kg at rest, e=100% (readouts: 1.00 m/s left and 2.00 m/s right -> student writes -1.00 and +2.00; p=+3.00 kg*m/s, KE=4.50 J before and after).
Buzz fit:
- Fixed numeric ready. Buzz owns the whole grade through native questions; no SCORM package needed.
