13) In Level 4 the swimmer and the baby drift downstream at the same 3 m/s. Use relative motion to explain why the time it takes to reach the baby depends only on your swim speed, and why the current still decides whether the rescue succeeds.
a. A strong answer explains that subtracting the baby's velocity from the swimmer's velocity cancels the 3 m/s current they share, so in the water frame the baby is stationary and the swimmer closes the across-river gap at the swim speed alone, making the time to reach the baby the gap divided by the swim speed. It then explains that the ground frame still matters because both are drifting toward the lower rock while that happens, so too slow lets the current carry the baby into the rock first, and too fast leaves the swimmer too high in the river and into the upper boulder.
