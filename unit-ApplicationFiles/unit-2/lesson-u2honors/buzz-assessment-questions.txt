Buzz Assessment Questions: Unit 2 Honors Project - Coriolis Cannon Challenge

Assessment setup
- Student opens the hosted Coriolis Cannon Challenge from the Buzz directions.
- Student fires once with direct aim, then changes aim correction until the shell hits the target.
- Student compares short and long target distances, uploads evidence, and explains the Coriolis effect in a rotating frame.
- Suggested total: 12 points.
- Important teacher note: The current lab is exploratory, so numeric answers vary unless fixed Buzz presets are added.

Question 1: Direct-Aim Prediction
Type: Multiple choice or short answer
Points: 1
Prompt: Before firing, predict whether direct aim will hit the target, miss to one side, or need aim correction. Explain your prediction in one sentence.
Grading guide: Full credit includes a prediction and connects it to Earth rotation or the rotating frame.

Question 2: Direct-Aim Trial Data
Type: Short answer
Points: 1
Prompt: With aim correction set to 0 deg, record the target distance, shell speed, Earth rotation rate, observer latitude, hemisphere, and the hit/miss result.
Grading guide: Full credit if all settings and the result are included.

Question 3: Corrected Aim
Type: Numeric response or short answer
Points: 1
Prompt: Adjust the aim correction until the shell hits the target. Record the aim correction that produced a hit.
Grading guide: Full credit if the value matches the uploaded evidence.

Question 4: Hit Evidence
Type: File upload
Points: 2
Prompt: Upload a screenshot showing the hit result and the control settings you used.
Grading guide: Full credit if the screenshot clearly shows Status: Hit and the aim correction, shell speed, target distance, rotation rate, latitude, and hemisphere.

Question 5: Short vs. Long Distance
Type: Short answer
Points: 2
Prompt: Compare a shorter target distance with a longer target distance. Record the aim correction needed for each and describe which required more correction.
Grading guide: Full credit includes two distances, two aim corrections, and a correct comparison.

Question 6: Rotation Rate Effect
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: Explain how increasing the Earth rotation rate changes the miss distance or required aim correction.
Grading guide: Full credit explains that more rotation during the shell's flight causes a larger apparent curve in the rotating frame, so more correction is needed.

Question 7: Inertia and Rotating Frame Claim
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: Explain why the shell can move straight by inertia while the rotating-Earth view makes the path appear curved.
Grading guide: Full credit distinguishes the inertial path of the shell from the rotating reference frame of Earth/observer and connects that to the apparent Coriolis deflection.

Question 8: Hemisphere Comparison
Type: Essay
Points: 1
Prompt: Switch hemispheres and repeat one similar trial. How did the direction of the needed aim correction change?
Grading guide: Full credit identifies that the correction direction reverses when the rotation direction/hemisphere is reversed, with evidence from the trial.
