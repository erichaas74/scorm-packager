COMPLEMENTARY LAUNCH RANGES
UNIT 2 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Take control of a two-cannon circus act and find the angle pairs that make the performers land together! Then hunt for the launch angle that sends a projectile farthest.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Use complementary angles to predict equal projectile ranges.
- Use trial evidence to identify the maximum-range angle in an ideal model.
- Explain why real baseball trajectories differ from the no-drag model.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Complete the three Level 1 trials using the Buzz-generated Cannon 1 angles.
2. Adjust Cannon 2 until each act succeeds and record the matching angle.
3. In Level 2, launch at 30, 45, and 60 degrees and record each range.
4. Answer all 10 Buzz questions in order.
5. Use both complementary-angle and maximum-range evidence in the final responses.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.

ANSWER CHECKLIST
- Keep launch speed and launch/landing height the same when comparing complementary angles.
- Record the paired angles and ranges. Explain how horizontal speed and time in the air work together, not just that the angles add to 90 degrees.
- Separate the ideal-model result from real baseball flight. Describe how a specific real-world effect could change the range.

SUCCESS AND SCORING
- The assessment is worth 15 points: 10 auto-graded points plus 5 BusyBee points.
- The final two evidence responses are worth 2 points and 3 points.
- No file upload earns or replaces points.

- BusyBee scoring: Award points only in 0.5-point increments, including whole points, from 0 to the question maximum. Select a rubric-supported allowed score; never report hundredths such as 2.85/3. Do not average or percentage-scale rubric criteria into an off-grid score. A binary completion rubric still permits only 0 or full credit.

REVIEW-BASED GRADING GUIDANCE

Accept equivalent wording, reasonable rounding and simulation precision; do not demand repeated units already supplied by a blank or an explanation that the blank did not request. For written responses, credit correct concepts, methods and consistent follow-through. Deduct for the original error without repeatedly penalizing its correctly carried-forward consequences. A minor omission is not a wholly incorrect answer. Judge only requested evidence; a clear qualitative graph change is valid when the prompt allows it. Preserve deductions for materially wrong values, misconceptions and missing required work. Binary completion items remain binary. Check the published assignment version and its actual question numbers before grading.

Do not include scores, percentages, points earned or deducted, internal IDs, or em dashes. Question numbers and physics values are allowed. Never invent evidence, a mistake, or mastery.
