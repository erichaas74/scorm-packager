Buzz Setup: Unit 4 Lesson 1 IMA and AMA Lab

Open the Unit 4 Buzz scoring and publishing guide.

Package contents

  • index.html: standalone lab for local or hosted testing.
  • u4l1-simple-machine-buzz-assessment-template.html: self-contained Buzz assessment template.
  • u4l1-simple-machine-buzz-assessment-template-preview.html: local preview showing question placement.
  • buzz-assessment-questions.txt: ten Buzz-native questions worth 15 points.

Step 1: Create the Buzz assessment

  1. Create an assessment titled Unit 4 Lesson 1: IMA and AMA Lab.
  2. Open buzz-assessment-questions.txt and paste the complete contents into the Buzz text editor or Edit all questions screen.
  3. Confirm that Buzz creates ten questions worth 15 points: three multiple-choice, five numeric, and two essay questions.
  4. Confirm the grading split is 10 automatically graded points and 5 BusyBee rubric-graded essay points.
  5. Keep the questions in their original order. Each <a:question></a:question> slot pulls in the next question.

Turn off question randomization. The template labels and slots are arranged in question order. Do not use one question per page.

Step 2: Upload the template

  1. Upload u4l1-simple-machine-buzz-assessment-template.html as the assessment template.
  2. No separate simulation resource is required; the complete lab is embedded in the template.
  3. Preview as a student and confirm all four level tabs are immediately available; the assessment values are fixed at 20 kg and 1.5 m; all three trials run; six IMA/AMA entries must be checked in each level; and ten native questions appear.

Step 3: Student workflow

  1. Confirm the fixed assessment values: 20 kg load and 1.5 m lift height.
  2. Review the three configurations, then choose Run all 3 trials.
  3. Use the acquired input/output force and distance measurements to calculate IMA and AMA for Setups A, B, and C.
  4. Enter answers to the nearest hundredth and choose Check all calculations. Both ratios must be correct for a setup to be verified.
  5. Complete all three setups to unlock that machine's comparison summary. Repeat for the inclined plane, pulley, lever, and wheel and axle.
  6. Use the level-specific tables and verified ratios as evidence. The final two questions require one within-machine comparison and one two-machine CER.

Validation notes

  • The ramp length and angle are linked so every ramp trial reaches the selected shared height.
  • IMA is calculated from the distance ratio: input distance / output distance.
  • AMA is calculated from the force ratio: output force / measured input force.
  • Friction is included in measured input force, so AMA is lower than IMA in every displayed trial.
  • The application records completion only. Native Buzz questions determine the score.
  • Use the local preview before uploading to verify question placement. If Buzz strips inline scripts, host index.html and embed or link it while keeping the native Buzz questions in the assessment.
