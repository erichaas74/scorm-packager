Lab Format Notes: Unit 4 Lesson 2 - Which Track Is Fastest?

Current lab format
- Buzz-native prediction and investigation with four simultaneous carts on different paths.
- Live timer for each cart, speed labels at 25/50/75 percent markers, stacked energy bars, and velocity plus centripetal-acceleration vectors on curved tracks.
- Shared controls are gravity, mass, friction on/off, and wheel rotational inertia. Start (5 m) and finish (2 m) heights are locked so the height change is always the same.
- The track designer replaces the old dip-depth slider: every track is 13 node heights that students drag on the canvas, type into the node strip, or set from a shape preset. Node 0 and node 12 are locked; the rest run 0 to 6 m in 0.25 m steps.
- "Time it (no animation)" solves the current shapes and fills the results table immediately, so a shape can be tested for travel time without watching the race.
- Replay preserves the selected setup. Slow motion changes playback rate but not recorded physics time. A slow shape is played back faster than real time so the race still finishes on screen; the reported times are unaffected.
- Results include finish time, final speed, average speed, path length, and three marker speeds, with copy and CSV options.
- Eight Buzz questions cover prediction, fair test, data, equal-height energy, travel time, vectors, variable tests, and conclusion.

Physics model notes
- Node heights are joined by a monotone cubic curve, so the path is smooth and has a real curvature at every point but never overshoots a height a student set.
- Each track is discretized into 960 path samples. Arc length, friction work, speed, curvature, and elapsed time are calculated sample by sample.
- A cart carries a 0.5 m/s release push. A dead-level track is a legal shape, but the cart only creeps across it at that speed, and with friction on it stops. Both outcomes are reported as DNF rather than a fake time.
- Total kinetic energy is 0.5mv^2(1 + beta), where beta = I/(mr^2). Translational and rotational energy bars split this total.
- With friction off, v_final depends only on gravity, start-to-finish height change, and beta, so track shape and mass do not change final speed.
- Travel time depends on the complete speed history. A track that creates speed earlier can have a higher average speed.
- Rolling resistance follows the normal force, N = m(g*cos(theta) + v^2*kappa), integrated along the path. On a slope alone that reduces to mu*m*g*(horizontal distance), which would be identical for every track, so the curvature term is what makes a dip cost more than a gentle path.
- A deeper dip can reduce time at first, but enough added depth lengthens the path and can reverse the advantage.

Teacher and BusyBee grading notes
- Student results legitimately vary with settings. Grade tables for completeness, common setup, units, and internally consistent relationships.
- Mass changes energy values in joules but should not change ideal speed or time.
- Increased rotational inertia should reduce translational speed because more energy enters wheel rotation.
- Centripetal acceleration changes velocity direction on curves; it does not add mechanical energy.
- Suggested total is 25 points.
