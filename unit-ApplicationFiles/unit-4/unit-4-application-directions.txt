U4L1

SIX SIMPLE MACHINES WORK LAB
UNIT 4 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Use six adjustable machines to explain how mechanical advantage connects force, distance, work, and speed.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- Animated models of an inclined plane, lever, wedge, wheel and axle, pulley, and screw show how each machine moves a load.
- Changing a machine's settings changes its ideal mechanical advantage (IMA). Compare how much input force and input travel are needed to produce the same output force and distance.
- In the ideal model, reducing input force requires more input distance. Input work equals output work; a machine does not create energy.
- With input speed held constant, a larger IMA also means a lower output speed. Use the model and your calculations to connect this tradeoff to each machine.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Calculate ideal mechanical advantage from physical settings.
- Compare input force and distance while keeping ideal work constant.
- Use calculations from several machines to explain output speed.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Use the shared givens: output force 120 N, output distance 1.0 m, and input speed 1.0 m/s.
2. Work through inclined plane, lever, wedge, wheel and axle, pulley, and screw in order.
3. Compare the two settings named in each pair of questions. Calculate IMA, input force, input distance, ideal work, and output speed using the provided relationships.
4. Answer the two questions beneath each machine. Use the coarse and fine screw calculations and at least two other machines in your final responses.
5. Before submitting through Buzz, confirm that all 12 questions are answered and the final two responses cite your evidence.

U4L2

HOOKE'S LAW LAB
UNIT 4 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Measure spring stretch and use the force-extension relationship to determine an unknown mass.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A hanging mass stretches a spring. After the spring settles, the extension measures how far it has stretched from its unloaded position.
- Recorded measurements appear in a data table and on a force-versus-extension graph. For the spring's linear behavior, a larger applied force produces a proportionally larger extension: F = kx.
- The slope of the force-versus-extension graph is the spring constant, k. A larger k represents a stiffer spring.
- The mystery mass shows an extension without revealing its mass. Use the spring constant you determined and the balance of spring force and weight to calculate the unknown mass.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Organize five force-extension measurements with consistent units.
- Calculate a spring constant and compare it with a best-fit slope.
- Use Hooke's law and evidence to infer a mystery mass.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Predict the force-extension relationship.
2. Record all five unique known masses for Spring A, measuring extension to 0.001 m.
3. Calculate and enter a finite positive spring-constant estimate before selecting Calculate best-fit line.
4. Attach the mystery mass and calculate its mass using m = kx/g.
5. Answer the questions using your measurements, slope, fit quality, and mystery-mass result.
6. Before submitting through Buzz, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite your evidence.

LOOP-THE-LOOP ENERGY INVESTIGATION
UNIT 4 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Use release-height trials to explain when a cart maintains contact around a vertical loop.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A cart moves down a track and into a vertical loop. Gravitational potential energy changes into kinetic energy as the cart descends, and kinetic energy changes back into potential energy as it climbs.
- The required trials show how release height affects speed and whether the cart stays in contact with the track.
- Having enough energy to reach the top is only part of the requirement. The cart must also have enough speed for the inward acceleration needed to follow the loop.
- Contact evidence distinguishes a completed loop from a trial in which the cart leaves the track. A calculated speed at the top is not an observed on-track speed if the cart has already detached.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Apply conservation of energy to calculate speed.
- Distinguish sufficient energy from the force needed to maintain contact.
- Compare all three trials using numerical evidence.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Answer Question 1 and record the in-app prediction before running trials.
2. Use the fixed assessment conditions: mass 100 kg, radius 20 m, gravity 9.81 m/s2, and zero friction.
3. Run release heights of 45 m, 50 m, and 60 m and inspect the speed and contact evidence.
4. Calculate the requested energy and normal force. Do not treat a hypothetical top speed after detachment as an observed on-track speed.
5. Use all three trials in your final evidence-based conclusion.
6. Before submitting through Buzz, confirm that all 9 questions are answered and the final two responses cite your evidence.

WATER BOTTLE THERMAL TEST
UNIT 4 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Compare controlled cooling trials to explain how bottle construction and lids affect thermal-energy transfer.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- Temperature-versus-time curves and recorded temperatures show hot water cooling during a simulated 60-minute test.
- The four trials compare plastic, metal, and vacuum bottles with lids, plus a vacuum bottle without a lid.
- Comparing the three lidded bottles isolates the effect of bottle construction. Comparing the vacuum bottle with and without its lid isolates the lid's effect.
- The temperature changes provide evidence of thermal energy leaving the water. Use Q = mc(T_final - T_initial) for the water's energy change and (T_final - T_initial)/elapsed time for its average temperature-change rate; both are negative during cooling.
- Insulation slows thermal-energy transfer. Use your recorded results to explain which design retains heat most effectively over the test interval.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Read temperature evidence over a 60-minute interval.
- Calculate thermal-energy change and average cooling rate.
- Use controlled comparisons to evaluate insulation and lid effects.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Answer Question 1 and record which bottle you predict will keep the water hottest.
2. Run all four trials: plastic with lid, metal with lid, vacuum with lid, and vacuum without lid.
3. Read the recorded temperatures at 0, 15, 30, 45, and 60 minutes.
4. Calculate the requested energy change and average rate with correct signs and units.
5. Use specific temperature evidence to explain the effects of wall construction and the lid.
6. Before submitting through Buzz, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite your evidence.
