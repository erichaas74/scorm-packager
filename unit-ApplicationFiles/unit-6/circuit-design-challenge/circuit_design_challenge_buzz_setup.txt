CIRCUIT DESIGN CHALLENGE
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Wire a reliable three-room electrical system like an engineer! You will measure current and voltage, compare series and parallel circuits, test failures and switches, and defend a design that keeps every room working independently.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Apply Ohms law and equivalent resistance to series and parallel circuits.
- Use ammeter and voltmeter evidence to compare circuit arrangements.
- Defend a parallel household-style design while recognizing its current tradeoff.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Open PhET Circuit Construction Kit: DC using the link in the lab. Choose the Lab screen and keep it open beside the evidence notebook. Build with a 9.0 V battery and identical 10 ohm bulbs.
2. Predict, build, and measure for each of the eight challenges. Enter actual PhET readings in the notebook; measurements and wiring are not imported automatically.
3. Use the same battery voltage for series and parallel comparisons. Calculate total resistance from measured V/I, compare brightness, and test each bulb failure and all four switch combinations.
4. Build your final three-room circuit, measure its voltages and total current, and test each bulb failure and independent switch. Revise failed designs and record your wiring description. An optional screenshot is kept locally and included in printing.
5. Answer and submit all 10 questions in Buzz using your evidence. The notebook does not submit to Buzz. Text downloads and screenshots are optional personal records, not graded uploads.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.

SUCCESS AND SCORING
- The assessment is worth 15 points: 10 auto-graded points plus 5 BusyBee points.
- The final two evidence responses are worth 2 points and 3 points.
- Evidence downloads are optional records. No file upload earns or replaces points.

- BusyBee scoring: Award points only in 0.5-point increments, including whole points, from 0 to the question maximum. Select a rubric-supported allowed score; never report hundredths such as 2.85/3. Do not average or percentage-scale rubric criteria into an off-grid score. A binary completion rubric still permits only 0 or full credit.

7. Use u6l3-circuit-design-buzz-assessment-template-preview.html for the local placement check, never as the upload template.

PUBLISHING AND PREVIEW CHECKS
- The PhET link opens, the eight build challenges are available, and evidence fields accept student measurements.
- No evidence is auto-filled. Predictions, measurements, explanations, and the optional screenshot survive refresh when browser storage is available.
- Progress means tables are filled, not that wiring was verified. The report is optional; only native Buzz responses determine the score.
- Questions 1-10 appear in their matching labeled slots and the final two essay questions remain last.
- Keyboard navigation, copy/paste, text selection, and the context menu remain available.
- Teacher-test data is reset before students open the assessment.
- Use meter readings, not electron-animation speed, as quantitative evidence.
