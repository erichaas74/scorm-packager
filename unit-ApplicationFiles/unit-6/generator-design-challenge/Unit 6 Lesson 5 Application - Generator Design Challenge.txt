Unit 6 Lesson 5 Application: Generator Design Challenge
PhET Simulation: Generator
Lesson: Unit 6 Lesson 5 — Electromagnetic Induction


Goal
Investigate what controls the amount of electricity produced by a generator, then use your evidence to design the strongest generator you can.


The PhET simulation allows you to change the water flow/turbine speed, magnet strength, number of pickup-coil loops, and coil area while observing the bulb, voltmeter, electron flow, compass, and magnetic-field meter.


Part 1: Explore the Generator
Step 1: Start the Generator
1. Open the Generator simulation.
2. Turn on the faucet.
3. Watch the turbine, magnet, electrons, and light bulb.
4. Slowly increase the water flow.


Questions
1. What happens to the turbine as water flow increases?
2. What happens to the brightness of the bulb?
3. Does electricity appear when the magnet is stopped? What evidence supports your answer?
4. What appears to be necessary for the generator to produce electricity?

Step 5: Change Coil Area
Keep everything else constant and change the loop area.


Questions

13. Why would changing the amount of magnetic field passing through the coil affect the generated electricity?


Part 5: Bulb vs. Voltmeter
Step 6: Compare the Detectors
Run the generator first using the light bulb and then using the voltmeter. Observe several complete rotations.


Questions
14. What information can you determine from the bulb?
15. What additional information can you determine from the voltmeter?
16. Why does the voltmeter reading change direction as the magnet rotates?
17. What does this tell you about the direction of the induced current?

Teacher Note
The PhET Generator guide emphasizes that electricity is produced by changing magnetic flux through the pickup coil. The simulation models induced EMF and includes a simplified back-EMF drag on the turbine.


BUILD NOTE (this application)
This activity is a self-contained HTML replacement for the PhET Generator sim, so it runs in Buzz/Rise without Java or legacy-plugin issues. It keeps all 23 questions and the prediction step verbatim and adds two things the paper version cannot enforce:
- A three-row coil-area evidence table for Step 5, so question 12 rests on recorded data rather than memory.
- A controlled-variables guard: within a series the app records only the variable under test and blocks a trial when a held variable has drifted, offering to restore the series settings.
Model: EMF = N x d(phi)/dt with phi = B*A*cos(theta); peak EMF = loops x peak flux x angular speed. A simplified back-EMF drag slows the turbine as electrical load rises, matching the teacher note above.
