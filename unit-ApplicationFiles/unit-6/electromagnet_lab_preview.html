Question 9 Iron core evidence and controlled variables

Buzz Question 9 E - 3 points
Type: EScore: 3Height: 240
9) Use Trial 1 and Trial 3 as evidence. Name the one variable that changed and the variables that were held constant, report both lifting results, and explain what the iron core does to the magnetic field. Then explain why the measured current was the same in both trials.
A

Full credit: Identifies core material as the only changed variable and names the controlled variables (20 turns, 10 mm diameter, one cell, same wire length); reports both lifting values from the data table; explains that the iron core concentrates and greatly strengthens the magnetic field produced by the current in the coil; and explains that the current was unchanged because the core is not part of the electrical circuit, so the voltage and the resistance of the circuit stayed the same. Partial credit: Correctly compares the two trials and states that the iron core makes the magnet much stronger, but omits the controlled variables, the data evidence, or the reason the current did not change. No credit: Compares trials that differ by more than one variable, claims the iron core increased the current, or cites no data from the trials.

Question 10 Defend your optimized design

Buzz Question 10 E - 3 points
Type: EScore: 3Height: 260
10) Describe the design you submitted for the optimization challenge. Give the number of turns, core material, core diameter, number of cells, and wiring choice. Support the design with at least three measured values from your data table, then explain how the 250 cm wire limit and the 50 dollar budget forced you to trade one variable against another.
A

Full credit: States a complete design (turns, core material, diameter, cell count, series or parallel) that reached the 150 clip goal; cites at least three measured values such as wire used, measured current, build cost, and clips lifted; and explains a specific tradeoff, for example that more turns or a thicker core consumed wire and money, so the design had to give up turns, diameter, or cells to stay inside both limits. Partial credit: Gives the design and some data but describes the constraints only in general terms or cites fewer than three measured values. No credit: Gives no design details, cites no data from the table, or claims every variable could be maximized at the same time.
