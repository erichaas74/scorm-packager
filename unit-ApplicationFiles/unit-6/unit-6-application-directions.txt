U6L1

MILLIKAN OIL DROP INVESTIGATION
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Suspend tiny charged drops in midair and uncover one of physics’ most important patterns! You will balance electric and gravitational forces, calculate relative charge, and use whole-number evidence to reveal that electric charge comes in discrete packets.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A charged oil drop moves between electric plates. Adjusting the plate voltage changes the electric field and the electric force on the drop.
- The balance indicator identifies the condition in which the upward electric force equals the downward gravitational force, allowing the drop to remain suspended.
- This scaled model uses equal-weight drops and the relationship q/e = 200 V / balance voltage. A drop with more charge needs a smaller field, and therefore a lower voltage, to balance the same weight.
- The six recorded trials let you compare relative charges and look for whole-number multiples of a common charge unit. The model demonstrates charge quantization rather than reproducing every measurement in Millikan's original experiment.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Explain the force-balance condition for a suspended charged drop.
- Calculate relative charge from the model balance voltage.
- Use repeated whole-number results to support a claim about charge quantization.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Adjust the plate voltage until the balance indicator confirms that the electric and gravitational forces are balanced.
2. Record the balance voltage, atomize a new drop, and repeat until the evidence table contains six drops.
3. Compare the calculated q/e values and look for the whole-number pattern across the trials.
4. Answer all 10 Buzz questions in order and cite at least three evidence-table rows in the final responses.
5. Export the evidence CSV only if you want a personal record; it is not submitted or graded.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.

DYNAMIC ELECTRIC-FIELD MOTION LAB
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Become an electric-field navigator! You will arrange source charges, launch a positive test charge, and reshape its path using invisible fields and vector addition until it reaches each target.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- Movable positive and negative source charges create an electric field in the arena. A positive test charge is repelled by positive sources and attracted toward negative sources.
- Launching the test charge shows how its motion changes under the net electric force. The first two challenges isolate the effect of a single source; the third combines sources to guide the charge around a barrier.
- Fields from multiple sources add as vectors. Changing a source's sign or position changes the net field and can change the resulting path.
- Electric force determines acceleration, not necessarily the direction of an already-moving charge's velocity. A curved path shows how successive changes in velocity can steer the charge toward a target.
- Saved source signs and positions provide evidence for explaining successful arrangements in the final questions.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Predict electric-field direction around positive and negative source charges.
- Connect electric field, force, acceleration, and changing velocity.
- Use vector addition to explain a curved path created by multiple charges.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Choose a challenge. Click + or minus on the arena to add a source, then drag it into place. Use [ and ] to select sources and arrow keys to move them with the keyboard.
2. Solve the first two one-charge challenges by choosing the correct source-charge sign and location.
3. Complete Challenge 3 by combining fields to guide the test charge around the barrier.
4. Use the force-arrow images to identify the mystery charge signs in Questions 1-2. Predict initial motion from rest in Questions 3 and 6, calculate in Questions 4-5, and explain your lab evidence in Questions 7-8.
5. Use the saved signs and positions beside Questions 7-8 in your final responses. Positions are percentages measured from the left and top of the arena.

CIRCUIT DESIGN CHALLENGE
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Wire a reliable three-room electrical system like an engineer! You will measure current and voltage, compare series and parallel circuits, test failures and switches, and defend a design that keeps every room working independently.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A circuit-building workspace lets you connect bulbs, switches, and meters to a battery. Ammeter and voltmeter readings show current through a path and voltage across a component.
- Series circuits provide one current path. Opening that path through a switch or bulb failure interrupts current throughout the series circuit.
- Parallel circuits provide separate branches across the supply. Opening one branch can leave the other branches operating, which supports independent room controls.
- Meter readings let you connect voltage, current, and equivalent resistance using V = IR. Adding parallel loads lowers equivalent resistance and increases total current for a fixed supply voltage.
- Failure tests, switch combinations, and the final three-room design show how circuit arrangement affects reliability and control, along with the increased current demand of parallel loads.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Apply Ohms law and equivalent resistance to series and parallel circuits.
- Use ammeter and voltmeter evidence to compare circuit arrangements.
- Defend a parallel household-style design while recognizing its current tradeoff.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Complete the eight circuit missions in order and avoid creating a direct short across the battery.
2. Place the ammeter in series and the voltmeter across a component, then record model evidence for each mission.
3. Test series and parallel bulb failures and all four switch combinations.
4. Build the final three-room circuit and confirm that every design requirement passes.
5. Answer all 10 Buzz questions using the evidence tables. Any downloaded report is optional and not graded.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.

ELECTROMAGNET DESIGN LAB
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Find the highest lifting strength per unit of cost. Calculate ratios, compare designs, and justify the best value using measured data.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A powered coil acts as an electromagnet. The required setups let you compare how current, coil turns, and core material affect the number of clips lifted in the model.
- Each test displays lifting strength and cost. Calculating clips divided by cost turns those observations into a measure of value, so the strongest design is not automatically the best value.
- Trials 1-5 provide fixed comparisons and checked ratio calculations. Completing them unlocks an optimization challenge with adjustable designs.
- Trial 6 shows how wire and budget limits constrain an engineering choice. Compare at least three distinct designs using strength-to-cost ratios and percentage improvement over the baseline.
- Saved design comparisons provide numerical evidence for defending the best-value design within the allowed resources.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Explain how current, coil turns, and core material affect an electromagnet.
- Design controlled-variable trials and interpret current and lifting data.
- Calculate strength-to-cost ratios and percentage improvement, then optimize value.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB

5. Review saved results and checked design comparisons, then answer the 10 Buzz questions. No evidence upload is required.

GENERATOR DESIGN CHALLENGE
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Turn motion into electricity and chase the brightest possible generator! You will control water flow, magnet strength, coil loops, and coil area while watching Faraday’s law come alive in the turbine, voltmeter, and bulb.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- Water-powered or hand-driven rotation changes magnetic flux through the generator coil. The voltmeter and bulb show the resulting electrical output.
- The voltmeter reading alternates as the direction of the magnetic-flux change reverses. A stationary magnet does not produce sustained output when the flux through the coil stays constant.
- Controlled changes to water flow, magnet strength, coil loops, and coil area show how the rate of flux change and the number of loops affect induced EMF.
- Evidence cards record RPM, peak EMF, and brightness so you can compare trials. Peak EMF is the largest voltage magnitude during a cycle, rather than the instantaneous meter reading.
- The final design demonstrates conversion of mechanical energy into electrical energy. Electrical loading can reduce turbine speed, creating a tradeoff when you try to maximize output.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Explain why changing magnetic flux produces an induced EMF.
- Use controlled-variable trials to identify how generator settings affect output.
- Optimize generator output while recognizing the load-speed tradeoff.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Start the turbine, compare powered rotation with hand rotation, and observe the alternating voltmeter reading.
2. Change only one variable at a time while testing water flow, magnet strength, coil loops, and coil area.
3. Record the required controlled-variable evidence cards and compare RPM, peak EMF, and brightness.
4. Build and record a final high-output design, then answer all 10 Buzz questions in order.
5. Use two controlled series and the final design in the written responses; no file upload is graded.

HONORS ELECTRIC MOTOR ENGINEERING CHALLENGE
UNIT 6 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Engineer a motor for a real performance goal! You will make magnetic force produce torque, test controlled design changes, and optimize speed, load, or power without violating current, heat, mass, and resource constraints.

WHAT THE LAB WILL SHOW
- A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences forces that produce torque. The motor converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation.
- The commutator reverses coil current at the appropriate part of each turn so the magnetic torque continues to support rotation in the same overall direction.
- Changing motor settings affects torque, speed, current, and useful output. As the motor turns, back EMF opposes the applied voltage and affects the current.
- Baseline and controlled trials show how design changes interact with load, resistance, friction, and heating. A change that improves one performance measure can increase resource use or violate another constraint.
- The saved optimized design can be compared with the baseline using the selected task's performance goal and its current, heat, mass, and resource limits.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Explain magnetic torque and the role of the commutator in continuous rotation.
- Analyze energy transfers, back EMF, resistance, current, and mechanical losses.
- Use systematic trial evidence to justify an optimized motor design.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Choose an engineering task and adjust the motor until every baseline constraint passes.
2. Save the baseline, select two investigation variables, and change only one selected variable between trials.
3. Record at least four systematic trials that test both selected variables.
4. Create and save a valid optimized design, then compare it quantitatively with the baseline.
5. Answer all 10 Buzz questions using the trial table and design log. CSV export is optional and not graded.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.
