@[Always] Your response is graded on judging the claim, citing evidence from both of your tables, and connecting the light arriving at the eye to the color a person perceives.
a. Full credit: Judges the claim incorrect; uses the filter table to show that light decreased rather than increased, and that the filter removed non-matching wavelengths; contrasts this with the RGB table, where combining bulbs genuinely adds light and produces brighter results such as white from all three; and closes by stating that the mixture of wavelengths arriving at the eye determines which cone types are stimulated and therefore the perceived color.
   Partial credit: Judges the claim incorrect and gives evidence from one table only, or explains the filter correctly but does not connect the light reaching the eye to perceived color.
   No credit: Accepts the claim, cites no data, or reverses the roles of adding and removing light.
