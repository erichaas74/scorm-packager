A stopped pipe has a node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end, so only odd multiples of the fundamental exist - the next available mode after 220 Hz is 660 Hz.

B

The pipe was too short for a 440 Hz standing wave to fit inside it.

7) In Challenge 3 all three strings had the same length and vibrated in their 1st harmonic, so all three carried the same wavelength of 1.60 m, yet they sounded at different pitches. What explains the difference?
A

Each string carries the wave at a different speed because their masses per metre differ, and frequency is the wave speed divided by the wavelength.

B

Each string was actually vibrating in a different harmonic, so the wavelengths were not really equal.

C

The thicker strings stretch and hold a longer wavelength than the thin ones.

D

The pitch of a string depends only on its tension, not on the wave that travels along it.

Question 9 What the warm room changed

Buzz Question 9 E - 3 points
Type: EScore: 3Height: 260
9) Report your recorded pipe lengths for Challenge 4 and Challenge 7. The target note was the same in both, and the pipe still had two open ends. Explain what the warmer room changed, why that forced you to change the length, and in which direction.
Always Your response is graded on reporting both recorded lengths, identifying what the warmer air changed, and explaining why that required a longer pipe.
A

Full credit: Reports both recorded lengths and notes the second is longer; states that warmer air raises the speed of sound while the boundary conditions and the fraction of a wavelength held are unchanged; and explains that since frequency depends on wave speed divided by twice the length, a larger speed at the old length would sound sharp, so the pipe had to be lengthened to bring the frequency back down to 330 Hz.

No credit: Claims the boundary conditions changed, claims the pipe should be shortened, or cites no recorded lengths.
