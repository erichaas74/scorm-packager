7) Explain why the spacing between the absorption lines told you which element you were looking at, while the position of those same lines told you how the object is moving. Use your own measurements from at least two of the three objects as evidence.

a. Full credit: States that each element produces a fixed pattern of lines whose relative spacing is set by the atom itself and does not change with motion, so matching the pattern identifies the element; states that motion shifts every line by the same fractional amount, so the whole pattern slides while keeping its shape, and the size of that slide gives the speed; and cites recorded rest wavelengths and shifts from at least two objects.

   No credit: Reverses the two roles, cites no data, or claims motion changes which element is present.

8) Object C produced a shift of essentially zero. State what that measurement does tell you about Object C's motion and what it does not tell you, and explain the difference.
@[Always] Your response is graded on stating what a zero shift does establish, what it does not establish, and why this method sees only one component of the motion.
a. Full credit: States that a zero shift means there is no measurable motion along the line of sight, so Object C is neither approaching nor receding from Earth; states that it does NOT mean the object is stationary, because motion perpendicular to the line of sight produces no first-order Doppler shift; and explains that this method measures only the radial component of velocity.
   Partial credit: Correctly says there is no motion toward or away from Earth but does not recognize that transverse motion would be invisible to this measurement.
   No credit: Concludes that Object C is motionless, or that the measurement failed.
