THIN LENS INVESTIGATION
UNIT 5 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Make light rays reveal an invisible rule! With the object fixed for each trial, move the screen around a converging lens, observe real and virtual images, and test whether the thin-lens equation predicts what the simulation shows.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Distinguish real images from virtual images using ray behavior and screen evidence.
- Apply the thin-lens equation and magnification relationship with correct signs.
- Use multiple trials to explain how object distance changes image position, size, and orientation.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Work through the four separate canvases. Each fixes the object at a different distance; drag its screen or use its own slider.
2. Answer image height, signed image distance, focal length, magnification, and orientation directly below each canvas. For L4 infer virtual-image values using the focal length found from L1-L3.
3. Write the final conclusion about the common focal length and real versus virtual images.
4. Use the four trials as numerical and operational evidence in your conclusion.
5. Submit all chart entries and written responses through Buzz; no downloads or separate notebook are required.
6. Before submitting, confirm that you answered all 21 questions and that your conclusion cites your trial evidence.

SUCCESS AND SCORING
- The assessment is worth 15 points: 10 auto-graded points plus 5 BusyBee points.
- Questions 1-20 are worth 0.5 points each (10 points); the final conclusion is worth 5 points.
- Enter and submit every response in Buzz. No evidence download or upload is required.

- BusyBee scoring: Award points only in 0.5-point increments, including whole points, from 0 to the question maximum. Select a rubric-supported allowed score; never report hundredths such as 2.85/3. Do not average or percentage-scale rubric criteria into an off-grid score. A binary completion rubric still permits only 0 or full credit.
