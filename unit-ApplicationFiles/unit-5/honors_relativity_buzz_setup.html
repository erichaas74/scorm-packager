Buzz Setup: Honors Relativity Timekeeping Lab

What this activity is

A self-contained honors investigation comparing a satellite clock with a ground atomic clock. Students choose an altitude and a speed (or use a preset, or Snap to circular orbit), read the gravity effect, the motion effect, and the net drift, and record trials into a data table. Their trials plot as points against the theoretical circular-orbit curve on the drift-versus-radius graph. Six evidence cards describe non-GPS tests of the same physics.

The lab's eight built-in analysis questions were removed in the conversion, along with the two-attempt logic, the running score badge, and the five-step teaching walkthrough. Every one of those carried a fully worked explanation that answered the graded questions before they were asked. Buzz now owns the assessment.

The existing unlock gate was kept but repurposed: four trials including one fast clock and one slow clock now unlock the evidence image rather than an internal question set.

1. Import the 9 questions

  1. Create a Buzz Assessment titled Unit 5 Honors: Relativity Timekeeping Lab.
  2. Paste all of honors_relativity_buzz_questions.txt into Buzz's Text editor / Edit all questions screen.
  3. Confirm the import creates 9 questions worth 16 points: one upload, three numeric, three multiple choice, and two essays.

2. Upload the assessment template

  1. Upload honors-relativity-timekeeping-lab/index.html as the assessment template.
  2. The template contains exactly 9 ordered <a:question /> markers.
  3. Turn off question randomization/shuffling and do not use one-question-per-page.

If Buzz strips the embedded script, host the complete HTML and use a teacher-approved external link. Do not simplify the orbital scene, the clock dials, or the graph to reduce file size.

3. What the readouts do and do not show

The GR, SR, and net-drift readouts are the lab's measuring instrument and remain visible — students read them the way they would read a meter. The Uncorrected GPS error readout and the Error column in the data table were removed, because Question 3 asks the student to calculate exactly that value. The formula subtitles under the GR and SR cards were also removed; deriving the relationship is part of the honors work.

Multiple choice

Q5: gravity wins; the GPS clock runs fast (GR +45.7, SR −7.1, net about +38.6 µs/day).
Q6: the ISS clock runs slow (GR +3.7, SR −28.2, net about −24 µs/day).
Q7: speed is measured in the non-rotating Earth-centered frame — eastward is 465 + 250 m/s, westward is 465 − 250 m/s.

Upload scoring: Award the three Question 1 points when the evidence image shows at least four trials with at least one positive and one negative net drift.

5. Student preview checklist

  1. The orbital scene animates and both clock dials advance (script not stripped).
  2. Presets, both sliders, and Snap to circular orbit all change the readouts.
  3. Record four trials including one fast and one slow clock; the gate message turns green and the Download evidence image button unlocks.
  4. Recorded trials appear as points on the graph against the theoretical curve.
  5. Confirm Questions 1-9 appear in the matching labeled slots. If they do not, randomization is still on.
  6. Enter the key values above and confirm all three numeric questions grade correct.
  7. Confirm both essay boxes render and the upload question accepts PNG.
  8. Confirm the visible page title reads Honors Relativity Timekeeping Lab.
  9. Confirm copy and paste are blocked inside the Buzz question controls, not only in the lab body.
  10. Use Reset Lab after testing so students begin with an empty data table.
