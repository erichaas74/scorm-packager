Buzz Setup: Design the Perfect Instrument

What this activity is

A self-contained resonance builder with seven challenges. Students tune a clamped string and build open and stopped air columns until the on-screen tuner reaches a target note, then continue to the next challenge. Each successful build is recorded into a table with its length, tension, harmonic, air temperature, and achieved frequency.

The lab's 24 built-in multiple-choice questions were removed in the conversion, along with their worked feedback strings. Every prediction question stated the boundary conditions and the wavelength fraction before the student built anything, which is exactly what Questions 5 through 8 now grade. Buzz owns the assessment.

1. Import the 8 questions

  1. Create a Buzz Assessment titled Unit 5 Lesson 2: Design the Perfect Instrument.
  2. Paste all of design_perfect_instrument_buzz_questions.txt into Buzz's Text editor / Edit all questions screen.
  3. Confirm the import creates 8 questions worth 15 points: four numeric, two multiple choice, and two essays. The score split is 10 auto-graded points plus 5 BusyBee points.

2. Upload the assessment template

  1. Upload design-the-perfect-instrument/u5l2-perfect-instrument-buzz-assessment-template.html as the assessment template.
  2. The template contains exactly 8 ordered <a:question /> markers.
  3. Turn off question randomization/shuffling and do not use one-question-per-page.

If Buzz strips the embedded script, host the complete HTML and use a teacher-approved external link. The tuner uses Web Audio; confirm audio is permitted, but note every cue is also visual.

3. Why challenges 4 and 5 are locked to the fundamental

Those two challenges originally left the harmonic slider live. An open pipe at 1.04 m driven at n = 2 also produces E4, and a stopped pipe at 1.17 m driven at n = 3 also produces A3 — both inside the 1.20 m slider range. That made the graded length ambiguous, so enable for challenges 4 and 5 is now ["L"] and every student records the same fundamental length. Challenges 2 and 6 still expose the harmonic slider, because sweeping the modes is the point of those two.

4. Answer key

Physics the simulation uses

String wave speed v = √(T/μ) with μ = 0.001 kg/m, so T = 60 N gives 244.9 m/s. Sound speed v = 331 + 0.6 × T(°C), so 343 m/s at 20 °C and 352 m/s at 35 °C. A build locks in when the tuner is within the challenge's cents tolerance.

Multiple choice

Q5: node at the closed end and antinode at the open end allow only odd multiples, so the pipe jumps from 220 Hz to 660 Hz.
Q6: the three strings carry the wave at different speeds because their masses per metre differ, and f = v / λ.

Evidence policy: The build-evidence PNG is optional and ungraded. Students earn all points through the native questions while the completed build table remains on screen.

5. BusyBee essay grading

Both essays in this assessment (Questions 7 and 8) are graded by BusyBee. Each carries Meta-unit, Meta-lesson, Meta-skill, Meta-grading: busybee, and Meta-evidence tags, an @[Always] line telling the student what is being graded, and a Full/Partial/No credit rubric in the answer line.

The authoritative rubric copy is design_perfect_instrument-busybee-rubric-metadata.json in this folder. It carries the same rubric criteria plus per-question common misconceptions, revision-oriented feedback strings, and the human-review triggers. If Buzz ignores custom Meta- metadata in your domain, that JSON is the copy of record — load it into BusyBee directly.

Route to a human grader when the response conflicts with the on-screen build table, when reported values may be supported by the student's run, or when the reasoning is unusual but could be correct. The full trigger list is in the JSON.
