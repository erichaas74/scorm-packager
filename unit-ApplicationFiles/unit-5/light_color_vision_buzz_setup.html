Buzz Setup: Light, Color, and Vision Lab

What this activity is

A self-contained color and vision investigation with three workspaces (Single Bulb, Wavelength & Cones, RGB Bulbs) and an Evidence tab holding four data tables. Students record 18 evidence trials: four filter trials, seven RGB on/off combinations, three secondary-color builds, and four color-matching challenges.

The lab's 29-question written notebook was removed in the conversion. Those responses lived in browser storage where no teacher could reach them; the assessment is now owned entirely by Buzz. The evidence tables and the 18 recorded trials remain in the lab and feed the evidence image.

1. Import the 9 questions

  1. Create a Buzz Assessment titled Unit 5 Lesson 3: Light, Color, and Vision Lab.
  2. Paste all of light_color_vision_buzz_questions.txt into Buzz's Text editor / Edit all questions screen.
  3. Confirm the import creates 9 questions worth 18 points: one upload, one matching, two numeric, two multiple choice, and three essays.

2. Upload the assessment template

  1. Upload light-color-and-vision-lab/index.html as the assessment template.
  2. The template contains exactly 9 ordered <a:question /> markers.
  3. Turn off question randomization/shuffling and do not use one-question-per-page.

If Buzz strips the embedded script, host the complete HTML and use a teacher-approved external link. Do not simplify the beam, photon, or cone-response graphics to reduce file size.

Numeric grading ranges

The simulation's transmission model is fixed: white light through any filter transmits exactly 28%; monochromatic light through a filter transmits exp(−0.5 · ((λbulb − λfilter) / 34)2), which is 100% when the two match.

Multiple choice

Q5: the filter removes non-matching wavelengths and transmits the rest, so less light reaches the eye.
Q6: white light is a mixture of many visible wavelengths, not a single wavelength.
