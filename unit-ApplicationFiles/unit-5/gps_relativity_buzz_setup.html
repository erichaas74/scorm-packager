Buzz Setup: GPS and Relativity Investigation

What this activity is

A three-stage activity: a five-step explainer, a four-part investigation, and a concise evidence report delivered as the final two BusyBee questions. It answers one question: why does a satellite navigation system need relativity, and how do engineers cancel it?

The investigation runs on the real weak-field model, not on demonstration values. Students measure the gravitational and kinematic drift rates at the true GPS orbit, then find the pre-launch frequency offset that cancels the net effect — the offset that real GPS satellites carry.

The explainer deliberately contains no numbers. It teaches the mechanisms and the directions (gravity makes the satellite clock gain, motion makes it lose) and stops there. Every quantity the assessment grades is produced by the student's own measurements in Stage 2, so the explainer cannot leak the report.

3. The physics the simulation uses

Weak-field rate comparison of a clock at radius r moving at speed v against a clock on the rotating equator:

gravitational = (GM/c²)(1/RE − 1/r)   and   kinematic = (vground² − v²)/(2c²)

with GM = 3.986004418 × 1014 m³/s², c = 2.99792458 × 108 m/s, RE = 6 371 km, vground = 465 m/s. The orbit is locked to the real GPS constellation: 20 180 km altitude, 3 875 m/s.

These produce +45.71 and −7.11 µs/day, a net of +38.60 µs/day, and a required pre-launch offset of 4.570 mHz on the 10.23 MHz fundamental — that is, the oscillator is set to 10 229 999.995430 Hz before launch. This matches the published GPS value of 10.22999999543 MHz.

5. BusyBee essay grading

The final two evidence responses (Questions 8 and 9) are graded by BusyBee. Each carries Meta-unit, Meta-lesson, Meta-skill, Meta-grading: busybee, and Meta-evidence tags, an @[Always] line, and a Full/Partial/No credit rubric.

The authoritative rubric copy is gps_relativity-busybee-rubric-metadata.json. It carries the same criteria plus common misconceptions, revision-oriented feedback, and human-review triggers. If Buzz ignores custom Meta- metadata in your domain, load that JSON into BusyBee directly.

Both evidence responses require the student to quote recorded values, so route to a human grader whenever the quoted numbers disagree with the on-screen data sheet.

6. Student preview checklist

  1. The orbit animation runs and both clock dials advance (script not stripped).
  2. The explainer advances through all five steps and its last button opens the investigation.
  3. Part A rejects a wrong slider position and accepts one at the 10.0 m limit.
  4. Part B records all three configurations; the drift readout changes sign with the toggles.
  5. Part C locks only when the residual drift falls below 0.2 µs/day.
  6. Part D runs a full simulated day in about four seconds and reports the time-to-failure.
  7. The Download evidence image button unlocks only when all four parts are recorded.
  8. Confirm Questions 1-9 appear in the matching labeled slots. If they do not, randomization is still on.
  9. Enter the key values above and confirm all five numeric questions grade correct.
  10. Confirm both essay boxes render and the optional PNG download works.
  11. Confirm the visible page title reads GPS and Relativity Investigation.
  12. Confirm keyboard navigation, copy/paste, and the context menu remain available.
  13. Use Reset investigation after testing so students begin with an empty data sheet.
