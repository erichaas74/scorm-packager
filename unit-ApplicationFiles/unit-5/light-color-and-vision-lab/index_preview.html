Question 7 Evidence from your filter table

Buzz Question 7 E - 2 points
Type: EScore: 2Height: 220
7) Use at least two rows from your Part 3 filter table as evidence. Report the light before and after each filter, then explain what happened to the photons that did not match the filter.
Always Your response is graded on citing at least two rows from your own filter table and explaining what happened to the photons that did not match the filter.
A

Full credit: Reports the before and after entries for at least two filters from the student's own table; states that only the wavelengths matching the filter passed through; and explains that the remaining photons were absorbed by the filter rather than converted or reflected, so the light leaving the filter is a subset of the light that entered.

Partial credit: Cites the table and states that the filter blocked some light, but does not identify absorption or does not use two rows.

No credit: Cites no data, or claims the filter added light or changed one wavelength into another.

Question 8 Your gray and white matches

Buzz Question 8 E - 3 points
Type: EScore: 3Height: 240
8) Report the red, green, and blue values you recorded for your gray match and for your white match in Part 7. Explain what was the same and what was different about the two settings, and what that shows about how a perceived color is produced.
Always Your response is graded on reporting your own recorded RGB values for both matches and explaining what stayed the same and what changed between them.
A

Full credit: Reports both sets of recorded RGB values; identifies that the three channels were at or near equal intensities in both matches while the overall intensity was lower for gray; and concludes that perceived color depends on the relative proportions of the wavelengths reaching the eye, with overall brightness controlling how light or dark it appears.

Partial credit: Reports both settings and notices they are related, but explains the relationship only in general terms.

No credit: Reports no recorded values, or claims gray required a different set of primaries than white.

Question 9 How light reaching the eye sets perceived color

Buzz Question 9 E - 3 points
Type: EScore: 3Height: 260
9) A student claims: "A red filter turns white light red by adding red light to it." Use evidence from both your filter table and your RGB table to evaluate this claim, then explain in your own words how the light that reaches the eye determines the color a person perceives.
Always Your response is graded on judging the claim, citing evidence from both of your tables, and connecting the light arriving at the eye to the color a person perceives.
A

Full credit: Judges the claim incorrect; uses the filter table to show that light decreased rather than increased, and that the filter removed non-matching wavelengths; contrasts this with the RGB table, where combining bulbs genuinely adds light and produces brighter results such as white from all three; and closes by stating that the mixture of wavelengths arriving at the eye determines which cone types are stimulated and therefore the perceived color.

Partial credit: Judges the claim incorrect and gives evidence from one table only, or explains the filter correctly but does not connect the light reaching the eye to perceived color.

No credit: Accepts the claim, cites no data, or reverses the roles of adding and removing light.
