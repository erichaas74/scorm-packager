Unit 5 Lesson 3 Application: Color Vision Lab


PhET Simulation: Color Vision
Simulation Link: https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/color-vision
Lesson: Unit 5 Lesson 3 – Electromagnetic Waves, Visible Light, and Radiation


Goal
Investigate how the human eye perceives visible light, determine how red, green, and blue light combine to create other colors, and explore how filters remove wavelengths from light.


The simulation has two main sections: Single Bulb for color perception and filtering, and RGB Bulbs for additive color mixing.


Part 1: Single-Color Light


Step 1: Explore Monochromatic Light
1. Open Single Bulb.
2. Select monochromatic light.
3. Turn on the photon view.
4. Slowly move the bulb-color control through the visible spectrum.
5. Observe the photons and the color perceived by the person.


Questions
1. How does changing the light source change the color perceived?
2. What relationship do you observe between the photons reaching the eye and the perceived color?
3. Can one wavelength of visible light produce every color humans perceive? Explain from your observations.


Part 2: White Light


Step 2: Switch to White Light
1. Change the bulb from monochromatic light to white light.
2. Observe the photons traveling toward the eye.
3. Compare this with your observations of monochromatic light.


Questions
4. How is the photon mixture for white light different from monochromatic light?
5. What color does the brain perceive when many visible wavelengths reach the eye together?
6. Based on your evidence, is white light a single wavelength? Explain.

Questions
7. What happens to the photons that do not match the filter?
8. Does a colored filter add its color to white light or remove other colors?
9. Use evidence from the simulation to explain why filters are described as subtractive.


Part 4: Matching Light and Filter Colors


Step 4: Test a Prediction
1. Return to monochromatic light.
2. Choose one bulb color.
3. Predict what will happen when a filter of approximately the same color is placed in front of it.
4. Test your prediction.
5. Now choose a very different filter color and test again.


Questions
10. What happens when the bulb and filter have similar colors?
11. Is all of the light blocked?
12. What happens when the bulb and filter colors are very different?
13. Explain your results using the idea that a filter transmits some wavelengths and blocks others.

15. What color results from red + blue?

17. What happens when red, green, and blue are combined?
18. How is this different from using a colored filter?

Questions
22. Which RGB combination produced orange?
23. How was producing gray different from producing white?
24. Can the same perceived color be produced using different combinations or intensities of light? What did you observe?


Part 8: Final Evidence Challenge


A student says:
“A red filter turns white light red by adding red light to it.”


Final Questions
25. Is the student's statement correct or incorrect?
26. Describe what actually happens to white light when it passes through a red filter.
27. Compare additive color mixing with subtractive filtering.
28. Why can red, green, and blue light combine to produce white light?
29. In 3–4 sentences, explain how the light reaching the eye determines the color that a person perceives.


Simulation Note
The PhET teacher guide notes that the displayed photon dots represent many photons rather than individual photons. It also notes that some displayed “purple” photons should be interpreted as violet because pure purple does not correspond to a single wavelength.
