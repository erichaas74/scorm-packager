Buzz Setup: Thin Lens Investigation

What this activity is

This self-contained Buzz investigation uses one fixed unknown converging lens with a 15.0 cm focal length. Every learner completes four lens observations and verifies three calculation rows, so the numeric questions have stable grading ranges. The HTML needs no external images, libraries, or hosting when Buzz preserves its embedded script.

Buzz auto-grades the five numeric questions and one multiple-choice question. The teacher reviews the upload and two essay responses using the included answer guides.

1. Import the 9 questions

  1. Create a Buzz Assessment titled Unit 5 Application: Thin Lens Investigation.
  2. Paste all of thin_lens_refraction_buzz_questions.txt into Buzz's Text editor / Edit all questions screen.
  3. Confirm that the import creates 9 questions worth 15 points: one upload, five numeric, one multiple choice, and two essays.

2. Upload the assessment template

  1. Upload thin_lens_refraction_investigation.html as the assessment template.
  2. The template contains exactly 9 ordered <a:question /> markers.
  3. Turn off question randomization/shuffling and do not use one-question-per-page. Buzz fills slots in display order.

If Buzz strips the embedded script, host the complete HTML and use a teacher-approved external link. Keep the fixed assessment lens and required trial values unchanged.

Upload scoring: Award the two Question 1 points when one legible evidence image shows all four lens observations and all three verified calculation rows.

4. BusyBee essay grading

All 2 essays in this assessment (Questions 6 and 7) are graded by BusyBee, not by hand. Each carries Meta-unit, Meta-lesson, Meta-skill, Meta-grading: busybee, and Meta-evidence tags, an @[Always] line telling the student what is being graded, and a Full/Partial/No credit rubric in the answer line.

The authoritative rubric copy is thin_lens_refraction-busybee-rubric-metadata.json in this folder. It carries the same rubric criteria plus per-question common misconceptions, revision-oriented feedback strings, and the human-review triggers. If Buzz ignores custom Meta- metadata in your domain, that JSON is the copy of record — load it into BusyBee directly.

Route to a human grader when the response conflicts with the uploaded evidence image, when the student cites values outside the expected range that the image may nonetheless support, or when the reasoning is unusual but could be correct. The full trigger list is in the JSON.

5. Student preview checklist

  1. Record three focused real images and the p = 10.0 cm virtual-image trial. Confirm that the virtual row does not invent screen measurements.
  2. Verify all three calculation rows, reload once to confirm persistence, and download the PNG evidence image.
  3. Confirm Questions 1-9 appear in the matching labeled slots, the five numeric keys grade within the listed ranges, both essay response boxes render, and the upload question accepts PNG.
  4. Use the Reset entire lab button after testing so students begin with blank evidence.
