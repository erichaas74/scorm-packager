============================================================
PART A: Calculate Impulse from a Force-Time Graph (Figure 1)
Attach the Figure 1 screenshot to each question in this part.
Figure 1 setup: A 75 kg jumper pushes off a force plate zeroed to
body weight. The net-force graph is a triangle that rises from 0 N
at t = 0 s to a peak of 1500 N at t = 0.20 s, then falls back to
0 N at t = 0.40 s. Each grid box = 0.1 s x 500 N = 50 N*s.
============================================================

Question 1: Read the Push Time
Type: Numeric response
Points: 1
Prompt: Using Figure 1, how long does the push last? Read the time from where the curve leaves 0 N to where it returns to 0 N. Enter the time in seconds.
Answer: 0.40 s (accept 0.38-0.42)

Question 2: Average Net Force
Type: Numeric response
Points: 2
Prompt: Using Figure 1, determine the average net force during the push. Hint: for a triangle-shaped graph, the average force is half the peak force. Enter the answer in newtons.
Answer: 750 N (accept 700-800)

Question 3: Net Impulse from the Graph
Type: Numeric response
Points: 2
Prompt: Calculate the net impulse delivered during the push in Figure 1. Use J = F_avg x dt, or find the area under the graph (area of the triangle = 1/2 x base x height). Enter the impulse in N*s.
Answer: 300 N*s (accept 285-315)
Teacher note: Both methods give 300 N*s: 750 N x 0.40 s, or 1/2 x 0.40 s x 1500 N. Counting grid boxes also works (6 boxes x 50 N*s).

Question 4: Takeoff Velocity
Type: Numeric response
Points: 1
Prompt: The jumper in Figure 1 has a mass of 75 kg and starts from rest. Use the impulse-momentum theorem, J = m x (change in v), to find the takeoff velocity in m/s.
Answer: 4.0 m/s (accept 3.8-4.2)

Question 5: Maximum Jump Height
Type: Numeric response
Points: 1
Prompt: Use the takeoff velocity from Question 4 to find the maximum jump height with h = v^2 / (2g). Use g = 9.81 m/s^2. Enter the height in meters.
Answer: 0.82 m (accept 0.74-0.90)

PART C: Explain Impulse in Your Own Words
============================================================

Question 9: What Is Impulse?
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: In your own words, explain what impulse is and how you can find it from a force-time graph. Your answer should mention both quantities that impulse depends on.
Grading guide: Full credit defines impulse as force multiplied by the time the force acts (J = F x dt), identifies it as the area under the force-time graph, and states that it equals the change in momentum (J = m x change in v).

Question 10: Impulse vs. Peak Force
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: A student claims: "The jumper with the biggest peak force always jumps the highest." Use what you learned in the lab to explain why this claim is not always true.
Grading guide: Full credit explains that jump height depends on impulse (the area under the curve), not the peak alone; a lower force applied for a longer time can create an equal or larger impulse, so equal or greater takeoff velocity and height.

Question 11: Compare Jump Profiles
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: Compare Athletic Max Jump with either Quick Hop or Deep Squat Jump. Which produced a larger jump height, and what graph evidence explains the difference?
Grading guide: Full credit compares the area under each force-time curve (impulse) and connects the larger impulse to greater takeoff speed and greater height, citing values from the lab readouts or calculation history.

Question 12: Same Impulse, Different Push (Figure 2)
Type: Essay
Points: 3
Prompt: Figure 2 shows two pushes on the same force plate. Push A is short and strong; Push B is long and gentle. (a) Estimate and compare the impulse of each push using the graph. (b) If both jumpers have the same mass, compare their takeoff velocities. (c) Give one real-world example where spreading a force over more time is useful.
Grading guide: (a) Both areas are about 180 N*s (4.5 grid boxes each at 40 N*s per box) - the impulses are equal, 1 pt. (b) Equal impulse and equal mass means equal takeoff velocity, 1 pt. (c) Any valid example - bending knees on landing, airbags, catching a ball with "soft hands", crumple zones - explained with force reduced because time increased, 1 pt.
