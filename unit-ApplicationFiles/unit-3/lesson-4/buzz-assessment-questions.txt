5) Analysis: Compare your before-and-after totals in the Trial 1 chart, then think about Trials 2-4 in the simulator (Trials 3 and 4 used 0% elasticity, where the balls stick together). (a) What happened to total momentum in every collision? (b) When was kinetic energy conserved and when was it lost? (c) Based on that, define an elastic collision versus an inelastic collision in your own words.
a. A strong answer states that total momentum was conserved in every trial (Trial 1: +3.00 kg*m/s before and after); kinetic energy was conserved only in the 100% elasticity trials (Trial 1: 4.50 J before and after) and decreased in the 0% trials where the balls stuck together; an elastic collision conserves both momentum and kinetic energy, while an inelastic collision conserves momentum but loses kinetic energy to heat, sound, and deformation.
