11) What did you notice about the total momentum before and after each collision?
a. A strong answer states that the total momentum after the collision equaled the total momentum before it in every trial - at 100%, 50%, and 0% elasticity, even when the balls stuck together. Momentum is conserved in every collision.

12) How can the total momentum be zero if the balls are moving?
a. A strong answer explains that momentum is a vector: in Trial 2, Ball 1's momentum was +3.00 kg*m/s (right) and Ball 2's was -3.00 kg*m/s (left), so the two momenta are equal in size but opposite in direction and cancel to zero. A system total of zero does not mean nothing is moving - it means the rightward and leftward momenta balance.

13) Did the total velocity remain the same before and after the collision?
a. A strong answer shows the sums are different (for example Trial 1: +3.0 + 0 = +3.0 m/s before, but -1.00 + 2.00 = +1.00 m/s after), so total velocity is NOT conserved. It is momentum - mass times velocity - that is conserved; the masses matter, so velocity alone does not balance.

14) Why do most things that collide slowly come to rest after a collision?
a. A strong answer explains that real collisions are mostly inelastic, so kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, and deformation, and that after the collision outside forces like friction keep acting on the objects, transferring their momentum to the Earth until they stop. Momentum is only conserved for the system while no outside force acts - friction is an outside force.
