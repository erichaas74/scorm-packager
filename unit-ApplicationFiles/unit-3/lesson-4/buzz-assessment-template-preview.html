Unit 3 Lesson 4: Collision Lab — Elastic & Inelastic Collisions

Run the collisions in the lab simulator, then fill in the data chart below. The chart boxes are real Buzz questions — enter your numeric answers right in the chart, then finish with the analysis question.

Learning Goals

  • Construct appropriate vector representations of "before-and-after" collisions.
  • Differentiate between elastic and perfectly inelastic collisions.
  • Discover when kinetic energy is conserved in collisions.

Direction matters! The simulator shows magnitudes with direction arrows only — it never shows + or − signs. You convert each direction to a sign yourself: right (→) = positive (+), left (←) = negative (−). Use p = mv (with your signs!) and KE = ½mv² (always positive). Round every answer to 2 decimal places.

How to collect your Trial 1 data

  1. Open the Collision Lab Simulator using the button below (it opens in a new tab).
  2. Stay on the Trial 1: Stationary Target tab. The starting conditions are fixed for you (shown in the simulator's setup bar and in the chart below): m₁ = 1.0 kg moving right at 3.0 m/s, m₂ = 2.0 kg at rest, elasticity 100%.
  3. Press Run Collision and watch the momentum arrows.
  4. Read the final velocities from the readouts. The readouts show a speed and a direction arrow — no sign. Write each one in the chart's yellow "record" cells as a signed velocity: → means +, ← means −.
  5. Calculate the four totals and enter each one in its chart question below. Afterward, run Trials 2–4 in the simulator — you will need them for the analysis question.
