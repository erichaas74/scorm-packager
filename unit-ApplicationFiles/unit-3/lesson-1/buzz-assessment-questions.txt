Buzz Assessment Questions: Unit 3 Lesson 1 - PhET Forces and Motion Lab

Assessment setup
- Student opens the PhET Forces and Motion: Basics simulation from the Buzz directions.
- Simulation link: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/forces-and-motion-basics/latest/forces-and-motion-basics_all.html
- Part 1: Student selects Net Force, checks Sum of Forces, Values, and Speed, then compares balanced and unbalanced pulling forces.
- Part 2: Student selects Motion, checks all display boxes, chooses the 200 kg refrigerator, applies force for a few seconds, then releases the force.
- Student uploads evidence from the PhET activity and answers the analysis questions in Buzz.
- Suggested total: 18 points.
- Teacher note: The provided key says "Sum of forces = 0" and "50 N to the right" for the first setup. Those cannot both be true. For the described two-small-blue-men vs. one-big-man setup, grade the observed net force/direction shown in the student's PhET screen.

Question 1: Net Force Setup Evidence
Type: File upload
Points: 2
Prompt: Upload a screenshot from the Net Force screen showing Sum of Forces, Values, and Speed turned on, with your pulling-force setup visible.
Grading guide: Full credit if the screenshot clearly shows the PhET Net Force screen, the selected display boxes, force values, and the cart/tug-of-war setup.

Question 2: Sum of Forces and Direction
Type: Short answer
Points: 2
Prompt: For the setup with two small blue people on the left side and one big person on the right side, what is the sum of the forces, and which direction does the net-force arrow point?
Grading guide: Full credit states the observed net force and direction from PhET. For the intended setup, the response should identify a nonzero net force toward the stronger side, commonly 50 N to the right depending on the selected people.

Question 3: Velocity During Net Force Motion
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: What do you notice about the velocity of the cart as the Net Force simulation proceeds?
Grading guide: Full credit explains that the cart's velocity changes/increases when there is a nonzero net force and that the motion changes in the direction of the net force.

Question 4: Term for Change in Velocity
Type: Short answer
Points: 1
Prompt: What is the correct physics term for a change in velocity?
Grading guide: Full credit: acceleration.

Question 5: Net Force and Acceleration Relationship
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: After trying balanced and unbalanced force combinations, what do you notice about how the change in velocity relates to the sum of forces?
Grading guide: Full credit explains that balanced forces create zero net force and no acceleration, while unbalanced forces create a nonzero net force and acceleration in the direction of that net force.

Question 6: Newton's Law for Net Force and Acceleration
Type: Short answer or Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: Which of Newton's Laws best describes the relationship between net force, mass, and acceleration? Explain briefly.
Grading guide: Full credit identifies Newton's Second Law and connects it to Fnet = ma. Also accept a First Law explanation only if the student is specifically discussing balanced forces or constant velocity when net force is zero.

Question 7: Applying Force to the 200 kg Refrigerator
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: On the Motion screen, what happens to the velocity of the 200 kg refrigerator while you are continuously applying a force?
Grading guide: Full credit explains that the refrigerator's speed/velocity increases while a nonzero applied force creates acceleration.

Question 8: After the Applied Force Stops
Type: Essay
Points: 2
Prompt: What happens to the velocity of the refrigerator when you stop applying the force?
Grading guide: Full credit explains that when the applied force returns to zero and the net force is zero, the refrigerator continues moving at approximately constant velocity.

Question 9: Newton's Laws Conclusion
Type: Essay
Points: 3
Prompt: Are your observations from the Net Force and Motion screens consistent with Newton's Laws of Motion? Explain using evidence from both parts of the lab.
Grading guide: Full credit connects Part 1 to balanced/unbalanced forces and acceleration, connects Part 2 to force causing acceleration and zero net force causing constant velocity, and correctly references Newton's First and/or Second Law.
