10) Watch the Section 4 flight with every readout switched off, then describe the motion in all four phases - burn, coast, free fall, and retro burn. For each phase state what the velocity is doing and what the acceleration is. Then explain why your answers to questions 8 and 9 are so different.
a. A strong answer walks through all four phases with both velocity and acceleration named. Burn: velocity grows from 0 to about 50 m/s while acceleration is constant at +20 m/s^2 upward. Coast: velocity drops from about 50 m/s to 0 at the apex near 187.5 m while acceleration is -10 m/s^2 downward. Free fall: velocity grows downward from 0 to about -50 m/s while acceleration stays -10 m/s^2. Retro burn: acceleration flips to +20 m/s^2 upward and brings the rocket from about -50 m/s to 0 at touchdown. The distance and displacement answers differ because distance adds up every metre traveled, about 187.5 m up plus 187.5 m down for roughly 375 m, while displacement measures only the straight-line change in position, and the rocket ends on the pad it started from, so the displacement is 0 m. The best answers also note that acceleration is downward and unchanged through both the coast and the free fall even though the direction of motion reverses at the apex, and that the rocket is momentarily at rest at the apex while still accelerating.
