Unit 1 Honors: Rocket Launch Lab Assessment

Four flights of the same mission, one per section. Each simulation shows only the values its flight computer can measure — every quantity you are asked for has been switched off on screen, so the answers have to be calculated, not read.

Work the sections in order. Section 4 flies the whole mission end to end with every readout dark.

Section 1 of 4

Powered Burn

The rocket lifts off from rest and holds a constant engine acceleration until cutoff, then the flight freezes. The grid lists only what the flight computer measures: the starting velocity, the burn acceleration, and the burn time. Displacement and burnout velocity stay blank — read the givens off the simulation and calculate them yourself.

Section 4 of 4

Full Mission

One uninterrupted flight: burn, coast, free fall, retro burn, touchdown. Every numeric readout is switched off for this run — no clock, no altitude, no velocity, no markers. Mission rule: the retro engine ignites at one third of the apex height, and the Retro row lists the net acceleration it produces, gravity already included. These questions are about the flight as a whole, so work out the free-fall and retro phases from that rule and then combine all four phases with your Section 1–3 answers.

Buzz Question 10 E - 4 points
Type: EScore: 4Height: 240
10) Watch the Section 4 flight with every readout switched off, then describe the motion in all four phases - burn, coast, free fall, and retro burn. For each phase state what the velocity is doing and what the acceleration is. Then explain why your answers to questions 8 and 9 are so different.
A

A strong answer walks through all four phases with both velocity and acceleration named. Burn: velocity grows from 0 to about 50 m/s while acceleration is constant at +20 m/s^2 upward. Coast: velocity drops from about 50 m/s to 0 at the apex near 187.5 m while acceleration is -10 m/s^2 downward. Free fall: velocity grows downward from 0 to about -50 m/s while acceleration stays -10 m/s^2. Retro burn: acceleration flips to +20 m/s^2 upward and brings the rocket from about -50 m/s to 0 at touchdown. The distance and displacement answers differ because distance adds up every metre traveled, about 187.5 m up plus 187.5 m down for roughly 375 m, while displacement measures only the straight-line change in position, and the rocket ends on the pad it started from, so the displacement is 0 m. The best answers also note that acceleration is downward and unchanged through both the coast and the free fall even though the direction of motion reverses at the apex, and that the rocket is momentarily at rest at the apex while still accelerating.
