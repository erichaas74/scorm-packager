Q6: Acceleration in Dive position

Buzz Question 6 MC - 3 points
Type: MCScore: 3
6) When you change from Neutral or Spread to Dive, what should your acceleration-time graph show while your downward speed increases toward the faster Dive terminal velocity?
When answer is correct Correct. Dive raises the target terminal velocity, so the diver has positive downward acceleration while speeding up.
When answer is incorrect Compare the target speeds: Dive has a higher downward terminal velocity than Neutral or Spread.
A

Zero acceleration immediately because body position never affects velocity.

A shape change alters drag and produces acceleration until a new terminal velocity is reached.
B

Positive acceleration that moves toward zero as the new Dive terminal velocity is approached.

Correct. Speed increases rapidly at first, then acceleration decreases as velocity approaches the Dive limit.
C

Constant negative acceleration for the rest of the fall.

Negative acceleration would reduce the downward velocity.
D

An upward velocity even though the diver is still falling.

Acceleration changes the downward speed; it does not instantly reverse the direction of motion.

The diver begins moving upward through the air.

Gravity turns off in Spread position.

Increased drag makes the downward velocity decrease toward the slower Spread terminal velocity.

Q8: Acceleration after parachute deployment

Buzz Question 8 MC - 3 points
Type: MCScore: 3
8) After the partner deploys a parachute, the acceleration graph shows a large negative value and the partner appears to rise in the tracking camera. Which explanation is correct?
When answer is correct Correct. The parachute sharply reduces downward velocity, so the still-faster observer moves downward relative to the partner.
When answer is incorrect Check both graphs: the partner's velocity remains downward while its magnitude decreases rapidly.
A

The parachute makes the partner reverse direction and fly upward.

The partner continues moving downward; only the relative camera view makes the motion appear upward.
B

The partner has zero acceleration because the parachute holds them still.

The opening parachute produces a strong negative acceleration while the downward speed decreases.
C

The student's camera moves upward faster than both divers.

The camera follows the divers; the apparent motion comes from their different downward velocities.
D

The partner slows downward much more rapidly, so the student continues downward past them and sees them move upward relative to the student.

Correct. This is relative motion, not an actual reversal of the partner's direction.

The partner changes shape but responds more slowly than you, giving you a chance to catch and match them. When their parachute opens, watch them appear to move upward relative to you even though both divers are still moving downward. The ground enters view only after deployment.
