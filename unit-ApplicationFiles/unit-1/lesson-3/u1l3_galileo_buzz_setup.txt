GALILEO ACCELERATION LAB
UNIT 1 BUZZ LAB GUIDE

YOUR MISSION
Recreate Galileo’s breakthrough with a digital falling object! You will measure equal time intervals, uncover odd- and square-number patterns, and turn a table of distances into evidence for constant acceleration.

WHAT YOU WILL DO AND LEARN
- Measure free-fall distance at fixed times and calculate interval distances.
- Recognize the odd-number and square-number patterns produced by constant acceleration.
- Evaluate experimental evidence and measurement uncertainty.

HOW TO COMPLETE THE LAB
1. Drop or scrub the ball to every marked time from 0.1 s through 0.7 s.
2. Complete the distance, interval-distance, interval-ratio, and total-ratio columns in order.
3. Answer Questions 1-8 using the fixed table values and patterns.
4. Cite at least two completed rows in Question 9.
5. Connect both mathematical patterns and one uncertainty source in Question 10.
6. Before submitting, confirm that all 10 questions are answered and the final two responses cite recorded evidence.

ANSWER CHECKLIST
- Use centimeters for distances and seconds for time. Read the marked time before recording a value.
- Keep the displayed precision while calculating; round your final answer. Reasonable ruler readings and rounding are accepted.
- Separate total distance from distance during one interval. In the conclusion, discuss both patterns and explain the effect of one measurement uncertainty.

SUCCESS AND SCORING
- The assessment is worth 15 points: 10 auto-graded points plus 5 BusyBee points.
- The final 2 evidence response(s) are worth 2 and 3 points.
- This lesson uses 10 automatic points because students complete the provided Galileo data chart.
- No file upload earns or replaces points.

- BusyBee scoring: Award points only in 0.5-point increments, including whole points, from 0 to the question maximum. Select a rubric-supported allowed score; never report hundredths such as 2.85/3. Do not average or percentage-scale rubric criteria into an off-grid score. A binary completion rubric still permits only 0 or full credit.

REVIEW-BASED GRADING GUIDANCE
Grade the understanding shown. Accept equivalent wording, reasonable rounding and simulation precision; do not demand repeated units already supplied by a blank or an explanation that the blank did not request. For written responses, credit correct concepts, methods and consistent follow-through. Deduct for the original error without repeatedly penalizing its correctly carried-forward consequences. A minor omission is not a wholly incorrect answer. Judge only requested evidence; a clear qualitative graph change is valid when the prompt allows it. Preserve deductions for materially wrong values, misconceptions and missing required work. Binary completion items remain binary. Check the published assignment version and its actual question numbers before grading.
Begin the overall comment with varied, brief encouragement naming only skills demonstrated in this submission. Then address only missed items, using the actual Question N label and one short correction per item, at most 25 words excluding the label. End with a freshly written positive sentence specific to this student's work; do not recycle a standard closing or use "Keep up the effort." Do not include scores, percentages, points earned or deducted, internal IDs, or em dashes. Question numbers and physics values are allowed. Never invent evidence, a mistake, or mastery. If every answer is correct, give only the evidence-based opening and specific closing. Per-question fields contain only the relevant correction; do not repeat the opening and closing in each field.
