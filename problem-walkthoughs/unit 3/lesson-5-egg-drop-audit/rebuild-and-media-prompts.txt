WHAT WAS REBUILT
The section starts with a concrete 1.25 m egg-drop calculation and develops the design reasoning from that result. Two native Process blocks now teach impact speed and stopping force. The first has four steps; the second has three. Four native LaTeX equation objects show the general speed equation, numerical speed calculation, momentum change, and paired stopping-force calculations. Each has an accessible description.

Six MC questions now cover speed, height versus clearance, air resistance, average net force, fair testing, and evidence-based redesign. All six intended answers were submitted in learner preview and returned Correct. All eight accordion panels were rewritten for the egg-drop assignment. The original four design goals remain in the design introduction. The previous decorative banner and unsupported prototype image/caption were removed. No new generated image, diagram, animation, or video was created or uploaded. The native equations are mathematical text rendered by Rise, not substitute illustrations.

The course overview was also shortened to name the egg-drop project explicitly and correct the original "conservation fo momentum" wording. The content of the other two Rise sections was not changed.

The numerical example is a teaching scenario, not a claim that a physical test was performed:
- Released from rest, upright, bottom 1.25 m above landing surface.
- Negligible air resistance for the initial prediction.
- Predicted speed: 4.95 m/s downward before first ground contact.
- Egg mass in the follow-up example: 0.0600 kg.
- Upward positive; initial velocity -4.95 m/s; final velocity 0; no rebound.
- Momentum change and net impulse: +0.297 kg m/s = +0.297 N s.
- Supplied stopping times: 0.020 s and 0.060 s.
- Average net forces: 14.85 N (about 15 N) and 4.95 N (about 5.0 N), upward.

SHARED VISUAL DIRECTION
Apply this paragraph before each still-image prompt:
"Create a restrained, scientifically accurate high-school physics teaching illustration. Use a clean, realistic studio cutaway style, a warm off-white background, natural cardboard and paper textures, and a limited navy/teal accent palette. Use one consistent egg-drop prototype: a small open corrugated-cardboard container, a white paper cup supporting one natural brown egg, soft folded paper around the egg, and a visibly compressible folded-cardboard base. Where the mechanism must be visible, use a clearly intentional cutaway rather than transparent or impossible materials. Keep the egg, cup, and container consistent between related images. Wide landscape composition, preferably 1600 x 900. Leave generous whitespace and use a simple visual hierarchy. No title, no repeated step heading, no equation, no worked calculation, no logo, no watermark, no decorative icons, no cartoon face. Only include the short labels explicitly requested. Do not imply that a generated illustration is a photo of a real student's test."

Numbers in an axis or measurement label are permitted where specified. Calculation expressions remain in Rise text. If generated text is unreliable, leave clean space for accurate labels to be added later. Reject attractive images that get the physics or geometry wrong.

STILL S01 - DROP HEIGHT
Placement: Process "Calculate the impact speed", Step 1 "Measure the drop height".
Suggested file: u3l5-speed-01-height.png
Prompt: Show a side-on view of the consistent upright egg-drop device at release above a flat horizontal floor. The egg stays secured inside its cup. Draw one straight vertical measurement arrow from the lowest external point of the device to the floor directly below it. Its exact label is "h = 1.25 m". Make the endpoints unambiguous. Add a faint outline of the same device at first floor contact, directly beneath the release position, to show the vertical displacement. Do not measure from the top of the device or from the egg to the floor. Do not show any compression yet, any push, a parachute, or a ladder. Keep the device size plausible relative to the drop height.
Suggested alt text: The device falls 1.25 m from its release position until its bottom first touches the floor.
Check: arrow begins at the device bottom, not at egg height.

STILL S04 - PREDICTION AND DRAG

Right: the same device attached securely by four taut strings to a modest deployed parachute, with a downward motion arrow and an upward arrow labeled "air resistance". Label the right panel "real fall with drag". The arrows should distinguish motion from force by shape or color. Suggested alt text: The ideal model omits air resistance; a deployed parachute adds upward drag during the real fall.

STILL F02 - EQUAL IMPULSE, DIFFERENT TIMES
Placement: Process "Compare the stopping forces", Step 2 "Calculate average net force".
Suggested file: u3l5-force-02-force-time.png
Prompt: Use a clean scientific graph style for this image rather than photorealism. Draw two side-by-side graphs with identical axes and scales. Vertical axis label: "average net force (N)"; horizontal axis: "time (s)". Left graph: a filled rectangular model pulse from 0 to 0.020 s at 14.85 N. Right graph: a filled rectangular model pulse from 0 to 0.060 s at 4.95 N. Use a common time axis ending at 0.080 s and common force axis ending at 16 N. The right rectangle must be exactly three times as wide and one third as high as the left, so areas are equal. Use small labels "short stop" and "long stop" and a discreet note "average-force models". Do not add a worked equation, claim these are measured traces, or show 15 N as a measured peak. No decorative egg illustrations are necessary here.
Suggested alt text: Two average-force rectangles have equal areas; the longer stop lasts three times as long and has one third the height.
Check: scales, units, rectangle ratios, and equal areas are essential. Correct generated axis text before use.
