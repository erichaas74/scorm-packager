Unit 5 Lesson 3 — Telescope & microscope

Group 1: animated demo. Original vector artwork, informed by the supplied
telescope and microscope references; no reference captions are instructions.
Two fixed thin-lens configurations, air, paraxial approximation. Ray colors
identify construction paths, not wavelengths. No student calculations or quiz.
The scene reveals incident rays, objective refraction, real image, eyepiece
refraction, and final viewing geometry. Each result holds before automatic
switching. Telescope/Microscope selectors and Replay restart the sequence.
Reduced motion displays the complete static construction for each example.
Completion is sent once after both examples' final evidence has been displayed.
Timers suspend while hidden. Replay does not clear the completion guard.
Playback: 1.4-second setup, 2.2 seconds per construction stage, 4.5-second
final hold (twice the original speed). Reduced-motion static views retain
their 16-second reading interval.

Telescope: objective x=300, f=260; eyepiece x=650, f=90.
Parallel incident rays have slope 0.12 in screen coordinates. Their common
intersection is (560,261.2), 31.2 below the axis. The objective's back focal
plane coincides with the eyepiece's front focal plane. All outgoing slopes
are -31.2/90. Final image at infinity: never draw a finite virtual-image tree.
Distant tree is an orientation inset, not a finite-distance ray source.

Microscope: object x=130, height=22; objective x=220, f=60.
1/f=1/do+1/di gives di=180, real image x=400, height=-44.
Eyepiece x=500, f=140: do=100, di=-350, final virtual image x=150,
height=-154. Two rays: parallel to axis then through objective back focus;
through objective center undeviated. At each lens, outgoing slope is incoming
slope minus (y-axis)/f. Dashed extensions of outgoing rays intersect at
(150,384). Solid rays always propagate left to right, even after crossing.
Final ant is inverted relative to specimen, upright relative to real image.

Scientific figure retains horizontal geometry on small screens in a labeled,
keyboard-focusable horizontal scrolling region. Instructions and controls
reflow independently; no whole-page horizontal overflow. Telescope uses a
detailed tree; microscope uses a segmented six-legged ant; both show lenses,
focal points, propagation arrowheads and a cross-section of the observing eye.
