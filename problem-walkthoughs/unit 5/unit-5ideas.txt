Unit 5 Teacher Simulation Walkthrough Ideas
Lesson 1: Wave Properties

1. Wave Anatomy and Medium
Interactive rope or spring wave. Teacher changes amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and wave type while labels identify crest, trough, equilibrium, compression, and rarefaction.

2. Wave Speed Equation
Teacher adjusts frequency and wavelength using sliders. The simulation calculates speed and visually shows that increasing frequency decreases wavelength when speed stays constant.

3. Compare Waves in Different Media
Send the same pulse through three connected materials with different densities or tensions. Compare wave speed, wavelength, reflection, and transmission at each boundary.

Lesson 2: Sound, Resonance, Harmonics, and Doppler Effect

1. Sound as a Mechanical Wave
Show a speaker vibrating air particles. Teacher can remove the medium, change particle spacing, or switch between air, water, and solids to demonstrate that sound requires matter.

2. Frequency, Pitch, Resonance, and Harmonics
Use a vibrating string or air-column simulation. Change length and tension, then highlight the fundamental frequency and the first few harmonic standing-wave patterns.

3. Doppler Effect Evidence
Animate a moving ambulance or race car emitting wavefronts. Pause before, beside, and after the observer to compare wavelength, detected frequency, and perceived pitch.

Lesson 3: EM Spectrum and Radiation Safety

1. Electromagnetic Spectrum
Teacher moves a selector from radio waves through gamma rays. Show changing wavelength, frequency, common sources, uses, and penetration ability.

2. Radiation Frequency and Energy
Display photons striking matter. Increase frequency while showing photon energy increase and the transition from non-ionizing to ionizing radiation.

3. Evaluate Radiation Claims
Present claims such as “microwaves make food radioactive” or “all radiation causes cancer.” Teacher reveals evidence cards and sorts each claim as supported, misleading, or false.

Lesson 4: Digital Waves and Information Transfer

1. Waves Carry Information
Teacher types a short message or selects an image. The simulation converts it into a changing wave signal, transmits it, and reconstructs the message at the receiver.

2. Digital vs. Analog Signals
Send identical information through noisy channels using analog and digital signals. Increase interference and compare how accurately each receiver reconstructs the original signal.

3. Technology Communication Model
Clickable communication system showing source, encoder, transmitter, channel, receiver, decoder, and output. Use a cell phone call, Wi-Fi message, or satellite transmission example.

Lesson 5: Wave-Particle Duality and Nuclear Models

1. Light as Wave and Particle
Toggle between double-slit interference and the photoelectric effect. Show why the wave model explains interference while the particle model explains photon-electron interactions.

2. Nuclear Processes and Energy Release
Animate alpha decay, beta decay, gamma emission, fission, and fusion. Track changes in protons, neutrons, elements, and released energy.

3. Model Evidence for Modern Physics
Teacher selects evidence such as interference patterns, emission spectra, the photoelectric effect, radioactive decay, or sunlight. Students predict which scientific model best explains each observation.

Lesson 1: Wave Properties
Does a larger amplitude make a wave travel faster?
Show two pulses with different amplitudes traveling through the same medium. They reach the end at the same time.
If frequency increases, does wave speed increase?
Keep the medium unchanged and increase frequency. Wave speed remains constant while wavelength decreases.
Does the material move across the screen with the wave?
Track one particle in the medium. It oscillates around its position while the disturbance travels forward.
Lesson 2: Sound, Resonance, Harmonics, and Doppler Effect
Does sound travel faster through empty space because nothing blocks it?
Gradually remove the particles. The sound weakens and disappears because sound requires a medium.
Does blowing harder into a tube create a higher pitch?
Increase the amplitude without changing the tube length. The sound becomes louder, but the fundamental pitch stays nearly the same.
Is the ambulance siren actually producing a higher frequency as it approaches?
Show the source frequency remaining constant while wavefronts compress in front and spread behind it.
Lesson 3: EM Spectrum and Radiation Safety
Are radio waves physically larger waves than visible light?
Compare wavelength, not wave height. Radio waves have longer wavelengths but are not necessarily larger in amplitude.
Do microwaves make food radioactive?
Show microwaves transferring energy to molecules without changing atomic nuclei. The radiation stops when the microwave turns off.
Is every type of electromagnetic radiation equally dangerous?
Compare photon energies. Visible light and radio waves are non-ionizing, while ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays can have enough energy to damage molecules.
Lesson 4: Digital Waves and Information Transfer
Is a digital signal a perfectly square wave while an analog signal is smooth?
Show that both travel as physical waves. “Digital” refers to how information is encoded, not the exact physical shape.
Does digital information remain perfect no matter how much noise is added?
Increase noise gradually. The message is recovered at first, but eventually bit errors occur after a threshold is crossed.
Does a stronger signal always carry more information?
Increase amplitude without changing the encoded pattern. The signal becomes easier to detect, but it does not contain additional information.
Lesson 5: Wave-Particle Duality and Nuclear Models
Does light switch between being a wave and being a particle?
Show the same light producing individual photon detections that gradually form an interference pattern. Both models describe different observed features.
Does a single photon travel through only one slit?
Send photons one at a time through a double slit. Individual impacts appear, but the combined pattern shows interference.
Does radioactive decay make matter disappear?
Animate alpha or beta decay. The original nucleus changes into a different nucleus while particles and energy leave the system.
