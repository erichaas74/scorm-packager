Cases and physics
- Toward: ambulance moves right toward the stationary listener on the right. Higher received pitch.
- Away: ambulance moves right away from the stationary listener on the left. Lower received pitch.
- Passenger: listener sits inside and moves with the ambulance at fixed separation from the siren. Same pitch.
- Source 600 Hz; sound speed 343 m/s in still air; vehicle speed 34.3 m/s. Received frequencies are 600/0.9 (display 667 Hz), 600/1.1 (display 545 Hz), and 600 Hz.
- Fixed audio gain. Compare pitch without a distance-dependent loudness change.
- Wave circles remain centered at emission positions, not attached to the moving vehicle. Display sound speed is ten times vehicle speed. Emission rate is slowed schematically for visibility.

Frequency comparison visual
- Reveal the compact source/listener comparison after the answer, alongside persistent feedback. Do not reintroduce waveform graphs.
- Red row: Siren, 600 Hz. Teal row: You, actual received Hz.
- Both rows use exactly the same 12 ms interval. Each curved mark represents one cycle after the common t=0 boundary. Spacing derives from the unrounded frequency.
- There are 7 source marks; the listener has 8 approaching, 6 receding, or 7 as passenger. Equal rows align. The displayed Hz, not the finite-window mark-count ratio, gives the frequency ratio.
