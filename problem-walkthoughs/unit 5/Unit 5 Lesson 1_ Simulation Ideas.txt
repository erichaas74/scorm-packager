Unit 5 Lesson 1: Wave Properties
Teacher Simulation Walkthrough Ideas


Part 1: Wave Anatomy and Medium
Learning goal: Identify wave features and explain how waves transfer energy through a medium.


Simulation ideas
• Interactive rope or spring wave: Change amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and wave type while labeling crest, trough, equilibrium, compression, and rarefaction.
• Particle tracker: Highlight one particle to show that the medium oscillates around an equilibrium position while the disturbance travels forward.
• Pulse versus periodic wave: Compare one disturbance that creates a pulse with repeated motion that creates a continuous wave.
• Periodic source model: Show how repeated source motion creates repeated wave cycles.
• Oscillator connection: Use a mass-spring model to connect periodic motion to wave production.
• Simple harmonic motion reveal: Track displacement, velocity, and acceleration during one cycle.
• Restoring force model: Show the force always pointing back toward equilibrium.
• Maximum speed reveal: Ask where the oscillator moves fastest, then reveal that speed is greatest at equilibrium.
• Maximum acceleration reveal: Ask where acceleration is greatest, then reveal that it is greatest at maximum displacement.


Prediction and misconception questions
• Does a larger-amplitude wave travel faster? Reveal two different-amplitude pulses arriving together in the same medium.
• Does the material move across the screen with the wave? Reveal one marked particle moving back and forth without traveling with the disturbance.
• Is a wave pulse the same as a continuous wave? Reveal that one disturbance creates one pulse, while repeated disturbances create a periodic wave.
• Where is an oscillating particle moving fastest: at the center or at the endpoints? Reveal maximum speed at equilibrium.
• Where is acceleration greatest: at equilibrium or at maximum displacement? Reveal maximum acceleration at the endpoints.


Part 2: Wave Speed Equation
Learning goal: Use v = λf and explain how frequency, wavelength, period, and medium are related.


Simulation ideas
• Frequency-wavelength sliders: Adjust frequency and wavelength while the simulation calculates wave speed.
• Constant-medium demonstration: Increase frequency while keeping the medium unchanged and show wavelength decreasing so speed remains constant.
• Period-frequency connection: Adjust frequency and automatically display period using T = 1/f.
• Cycle counter: Show frequency as cycles per second and period as time per cycle.
• Visual equation model: Place wavelength directly on the wave and show how many wavelengths pass a point each second.
• Same-speed comparison: Display several waves with different frequencies and wavelengths traveling through the same medium at equal speed.


Prediction and misconception questions
• If frequency increases, does wave speed increase? Reveal that speed stays constant in the same medium while wavelength decreases.
• If wavelength doubles in the same medium, what happens to frequency? Reveal that frequency is cut in half.
• Does a high-frequency wave always travel faster than a low-frequency wave? Reveal that the medium determines speed.
• Which wave has the shorter period: the high-frequency or low-frequency wave? Reveal that higher frequency means shorter period.


Part 3: Compare Waves in Different Media
Learning goal: Compare how waves reflect, transmit, refract, interfere, and diffract in different conditions.


Simulation ideas
• Connected-media pulse: Send the same pulse through materials with different densities or tensions and compare speed, wavelength, reflection, and transmission.
• Boundary reflection: Send a pulse toward a boundary and show part reflecting while part transmits.
• Fixed-end reflection: Reveal that a reflected pulse inverts at a fixed end.
• Free-end reflection: Reveal that a reflected pulse does not invert at a free end.
• Refraction model: Send a wave into a new medium and show speed and wavelength changing while frequency remains constant.
• Angle comparison: Compare a wave entering a new medium straight on with one entering at an angle. Only the angled wave changes direction.
• Constructive interference: Send two crests together and reveal a temporary larger amplitude.
• Destructive interference: Send a crest and trough together and reveal partial or complete cancellation.
• Superposition builder: Add the displacements of two waves point by point to create the resulting shape.
• Waves pass through each other: After overlap, show both pulses continuing unchanged.
• Diffraction through openings: Change opening width relative to wavelength and compare the amount of spreading.
• Diffraction around barriers: Compare long- and short-wavelength waves moving around the same obstacle.
• Four wave behaviors comparison: Test reflection, refraction, interference, and diffraction with water, sound, and light models.


Prediction and misconception questions
• When two pulses meet, do they collide and stop? Reveal that they temporarily combine and then continue through one another.
• Does destructive interference destroy energy? Reveal that the waves continue after overlap; cancellation is temporary.
• Does a pulse reflecting from every boundary invert? Compare fixed and free ends.
• When a wave enters a slower medium, does its frequency decrease? Reveal that frequency stays constant while speed and wavelength decrease.
• Does a wave always bend when it enters a new medium? Reveal that a wave entering along the normal changes speed but not direction.
• Which opening causes more diffraction: one much wider than the wavelength or one similar to the wavelength? Reveal greater spreading when the opening is similar to or smaller than the wavelength.


Recommended teacher walkthrough sequence
1. Create one pulse and track a particle in the medium.
2. Switch to a periodic source and identify amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and period.
3. Increase amplitude and test whether speed changes.
4. Increase frequency in the same medium and observe wavelength change.
5. Send a pulse into a second medium and compare speed, wavelength, reflection, and transmission.
6. Compare fixed-end and free-end reflections.
7. Overlap pulses to demonstrate constructive and destructive interference.
8. Send waves through different-sized openings to demonstrate diffraction.


Highest-priority counterintuitive reveals
• Larger amplitude does not make a wave travel faster in the same medium.
• Medium particles do not travel with the wave.
• Increasing frequency does not increase wave speed in an unchanged medium.
• Frequency stays constant when a wave enters a new medium.
• Reflected pulses invert only at fixed boundaries.
• Interfering waves pass through one another rather than permanently combining.
• A wave can change speed without changing direction when it enters along the normal.
