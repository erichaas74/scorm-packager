UNIT 5 LESSON 3: QUESTIONS AND VISUALS AUDIT
Reviewed October 11, 2026

Scope: Current live Rise authoring preview, all three sections of U5 L3 New Light, Optics, and Vision: Exploring the Fundamentals. Opened the accordion items and tabs, inspected process slide content and image elements, and visually spot-checked process rendering. No course edits or publishing.

MAIN FINDINGS
There are seven process blocks. Three older processes have no images on any of their six slides, for 18 text-only slides. Four newer worked examples each have six diagram images, for 24 populated diagram slots. These newer examples also contain equation graphics. The main need is to strengthen the older instruction and remove low-value repetition, not to recreate the newer worked examples.

1. HIGH PRIORITY: THREE IMAGE-FREE PROCESSES

Section 1: Introduction to Using the Speed of Light Equation
- All six slides are text-only: introduction, Identify the Given Values, Rearrange the Equation, Perform the Calculation, Interpret the Result, and summary.
- The instructions say to insert numbers but never work through a specific numerical case.
- Recommendation: replace this generic process with the existing illustrated "From wavelength to photon energy" worked example, placing it before independent practice. A second process is unnecessary unless it introduces a different reasoning task.

Section 2: Gather Your Materials / ray-diagram construction
- All six slides are text-only: Gather Your Materials; Draw the Lens and Axis; Add the Object and Focal Points; Draw the Rays; Find the Image; Review Your Diagram.
- Highest-priority image gap: a visual construction is taught entirely in prose.
- Recommendation: use a cumulative diagram. Add the axis and lens, object and focal points, parallel ray and refracted segment, central ray, intersection and inverted image, then a labeled completed diagram. State that this example uses an object outside the focal length. The later virtual-image worked example can show the contrasting case.

Section 3: How Your Brain Turns Light Into Color
- All six slides are text-only: introduction; Light Enters the Eye; Cone Cells Respond; Signals Sent to Brain; Brain Creates Color Perception; Color Perception is a Team Effort.
- Recommendation: show an eye cross-section and retina, overlapping cone responses, a simplified signal pathway, and a comparison of input light with perceived color. Reuse the same visual framework so each step adds information.

2. REDUNDANCY AND QUESTION QUALITY

I found five multiple-choice checks and three sorting activities. There were no verbatim duplicate multiple-choice stems. Redundancy is mainly repeated recall and repeated instructional blocks.

A. Repeated visible-spectrum coverage
- Section 1 has six color accordion items and "Order the Colors of Visible Light."
- Section 3 repeats all six color accordion items and asks "Which color of visible light has the shortest wavelength?"
- Recommendation: retain one full spectrum explanation. Make the later recall item an optional retrieval warm-up or replace it with transfer: predict an object's appearance under a different illumination spectrum and explain the reflected light.

B. RGB question adds little after the preceding activities
- Section 3 explains five mixing outcomes, sorts four input combinations by result, then asks "Which colors of light do screens use at the pixel level to create all other colors?"
- Recommendation: keep the matching practice, but replace the final RGB-identification item with a subpixel diagram asking what color appears when red and green are on and blue is off. A second case can vary relative brightness.

C. Definition repetition
- Section 2 immediately repeats the refraction/index/speed accordion topics in a three-card vocabulary block.
- Section 3 follows its color-blindness paragraph with cards that largely restate it.
- Recommendation: retain a compact optional vocabulary review, or replace the repeated cards with a diagram-based prediction. Not every repeated concept is redundant: the two lens multiple-choice questions assess different ideas and can remain.

D. Sorting interaction does not match ordering directions
- "Order the Colors of Visible Light" displays a single category, "Longest Wavelength to Shortest Wavelength."
- That category-sorting setup does not distinguish positions in a sequence.
- Recommendation: use a genuine sequencing interaction, or ask learners to choose a correctly ordered spectrum diagram. This is a configuration issue, not simply excessive repetition.

E. Limited independent application
- Current checks primarily ask learners to identify a spectrum region, lens type, focal-length strength, shortest-wavelength color, RGB primaries, device uses, or mixing results.
- The illustrated numerical examples appear after closing/transition prose and have no subsequent independent numerical check.
- Recommendation: move each worked example into its teaching sequence, then follow it with a different-number problem or image prediction. Keep a conceptual check about the meaning of the answer.

3. OTHER VISUAL GAPS

Section 1
- Frequency, Wavelength, and Energy tabs have no images. This is lower priority because a working frequency/wavelength/photon-energy interactive is already adjacent.
- Six visible-color accordion items are text-only here; Section 3 has illustrated versions. Consolidating them would remove repetition without adding six more images.
- "Key Regions of the Electromagnetic Spectrum" has flashcard-review instructions, but no flashcards appeared beneath that heading in the inspected preview. Verify the block or remove the orphaned heading/instructions.

Section 2
- Both Convex (Converging) Lens and Concave (Diverging) Lens tabs have zero image elements despite inviting the learner to see their effects. Add parallel-ray diagrams with focal points and dashed extensions for the diverging lens.
- Focal Point, Focal Length, and Real vs. Virtual Images accordion panels are text-only. One consistent labeled comparison diagram would be useful here.
- The three early Refraction / Refractive Index / Speed of Light panels DO have diagrams.

Section 3
- Red, Green, and Blue cone tabs have zero images. Prefer a shared graph showing three overlapping response curves rather than three unrelated pictures.
- All five additive-mixing accordion panels have no images. Add overlapping light patches or a shared RGB diagram, including a clearly labeled no-light case.
- Reflection/absorption examples have no instructional ray diagram showing source, object, absorbed wavelengths, and reflected light reaching the eye.
- Screens/pixels are described without a subpixel close-up. This is a good place to combine a new visual with a stronger assessment item.
- The six visible-color accordion panels DO have illustrations. Do not spend effort replacing these before the missing ray, cone, and mixing visuals.
