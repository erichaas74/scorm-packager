HIGH PRIORITY
1. Incorrect/ambiguous charge arithmetic instruction (section 1).
Current text: Adding positive numbers (protons) and subtracting negative ones (electrons) to find the net charge.
Subtracting a negative charge reverses the intended sign. Replace with Q = (Np - Ne)e, or add signed charges, +e per proton and -e per electron. Fix heading typo: 'of and object' to 'of an object'.

2. Contradictory electric/gravitational force ratio (section 3).
The proton-electron paragraph says 'a million billion billion billion times', about 10^33. The illustrated worked example correctly reports approximately 2.27 × 10^39 using its supplied constants. Use the latter consistently, with scientific notation.

3. Overgeneralized force explanations (section 3).
'Only objects with charge experience electric force' can imply neutral objects never do, contradicting the polarization case in the charge playground. Explain that neutral objects contain charges and can experience a net force through polarization in a nonuniform field.
The flashcard says electric forces are balanced with 'no net force', unlike gravity which 'acts unopposed'. Replace with: nearly balanced charge often reduces large-scale electric effects; gravity accumulates, but other forces can balance weight. A supported object is a simple counterexample to 'unopposed'.

ASSESSMENT AND SEQUENCE
4. Exact reuse of numbers and results reduces independent practice.
Section 2 MC uses +2 μC, -3 μC, 0.50 m and 0.216 N, exactly matching the following worked example. Its simultaneous-change question also repeats the worked example's doubled charges, halved separation, factor 16.
Section 3 MC uses +3, -2, +5 μC at 0.40 and 0.50 m, with 0.0225 N right, exactly matching the immediately following net-force worked example.
Recommendation: teach with the examples, then use changed numbers or a changed target/sign/distance for a genuinely independent check. Keep at least one qualitative direction prediction and one quantitative calculation.

5. Worked-example order.
Section 1 places 'In the next lesson...' before Count transferred electrons. Move that example before the transition and follow it with a different-number charge calculation.
Section 3 places the electric/gravity ratio worked example after net-force material and the balance challenge. Move it next to the proton-electron comparison; introduce net force afterward.
The numeric Coulomb and net-force MCs precede the corresponding complete examples. This can be deliberate pretesting, but they are not labeled as such. Prefer example then new-number practice, or label the initial attempt explicitly.

6. Lower-value recall repetition.
Section 2 asks whether +2 μC and +5 μC attract or repel after section 1 playground, matching activity, and sign instruction. Replace with a force-arrow/equal-force-magnitude prediction for unequal charges.
The five definition flashcards largely repeat equation/unit explanations. Keep as optional reference or shorten; this is lower priority than the exact repeated calculation scenarios.
The older eight-slide Coulomb process overlaps the six-slide numerical example. It has diagrams, so do not treat it as an image gap. Consolidate into a short checklist beside the concrete example rather than requiring two full processes for the same procedure.
