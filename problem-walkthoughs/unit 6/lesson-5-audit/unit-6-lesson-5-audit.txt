The largest problems are inconsistent physics between older text/questions and newer diagrams, an overemphasis on electromagnet cranes, and insufficient data interpretation and independent numerical practice. Preserve the goals and the two existing numerical worked examples.

HIGH PRIORITY FIXES

1. Separate electromagnetism, motor force, and electromagnetic induction.
Location: Section 3 opening, Reviewing the Building Blocks, Turning Principles into Practice, and Comparing Electromagnetic Devices introduction; section 2 How Microphones and Speakers Use Induction.
Problem: These passages say that motors, generators, and lifting electromagnets all rely on electromagnetic induction, and frame a speaker's operation as induction. A powered lifting electromagnet creates a field from supplied current. A moving-coil speaker uses magnetic force on a supplied current. A generator/dynamic microphone induces an emf through motion and changing flux linkage. Some motors also involve induction/back emf, but that does not make the present blanket explanation sound.
Fix: Use a three-way diagram: current -> magnetic field (crane), current + magnetic field -> force (motor/speaker), changing flux linkage -> emf (generator/microphone). State closed-circuit conditions when discussing induced current. Keep the crane only as a contrast or move it to Lesson 4.

2. Repair the largest-induced-current question.
Location: Section 1, question beginning Which setup will produce the highest induced current...
Verified key: 10 turns and large area; feedback says more turns and larger area both increase current.
Problem: Resistance is unspecified, and a magnet moving through coils does not establish equal uniform field change across differently sized loops. Larger loop area does not automatically capture a greater changing flux from a localized bar magnet.
Fix: Ask about average induced emf with explicit equal uniform dB/dt and orientation, or supply each circuit's resistance and flux change and calculate I = emf/R. Revise feedback as well as the stem.

3. Repair the motor-speed question.
Location: Section 2, Which change will make an electric motor spin faster?
Verified key: Increasing electric current; feedback guarantees higher speed.
Problem: Adjacent updated panels correctly explain that current affects torque under fixed conditions and speed depends on load/operating conditions. The quiz contradicts those panels.
Fix: Ask which change increases torque for fixed field, geometry, and angle. If asking about speed, define the actual motor, supply and load conditions.

4. Repair the overheating question and resistance explanation.
Location: Section 3, Wire Resistance panel and electromagnet question.
Verified feedback: Thicker wire reduces resistance and heat, allowing more current to flow without overheating. This is the best first adjustment to make.
Problem: This is not a general guarantee. At fixed current, P = I^2 R falls with R. For a simplified fixed-voltage resistive coil, P = V^2/R rises as R falls. Actual temperature also depends on dimensions and heat removal. The diagram's same-current qualification is missing from the prose and question.
Fix: Specify a regulated current and ask about resistive power, or supply measured current/temperature/lift data and a temperature limit. Remove the guaranteed no-overheating and best-first-adjustment wording. The generator-efficiency question can retain its direction but should specify comparable operating conditions and useful output.

5. Correct the design sorting activity.
Location: Section 3, Sorting Device Design Features.
Verified: Electric Generator accepts Relies on changing coil area for voltage. A conventional rigid rotating coil changes its orientation, not its physical area. Replace with Rotation changes magnetic flux through the coil and induces voltage.
Verified: Must manage heating from high current is assigned only to Electromagnetic Crane, although it also describes generators and other devices. Make the clue specific to lifting a ferromagnetic load, or permit multiple applicable categories.
Also review Section 2 sorting: Uses a commutator to reverse current needs a simple DC motor qualification; Relies on electromagnetic induction is not a universal separator of all motor types. Declare the device models used.

6. Align the design work with the generator assignment.
Location: Section 3, crane-centered Process and Evaluating Device Performance with Data.
Problem: The data section supplies general advice and a qualitative question, but no data table, graph, numeric constraints, or evidence-based decision. Section 3 has no embedded activity. The section 1 Electromagnet Investigation Lab measures field versus supplied current/coils rather than induced emf.
Fix: Move that electromagnet lab to Lesson 4. Move the Generator Optimization Challenge into section 3. Replace the six-step crane Process with a generator investigation: choose one variable, state controls, collect repeated/peak readings, compare a table/graph, evaluate a constrained design, write a claim supported by measurements. Explain that peak voltage, instantaneous signed voltage, bulb brightness, electrical power, and efficiency are different quantities.
The assignment's model includes load-related changes to turbine RPM. Teach students to record actual RPM as well as water-flow setting: equal water flow does not necessarily establish equal RPM when electrical loading changes. Distinguish a controlled model observation from an ideal fixed-speed proportionality.

MEDIUM PRIORITY FIXES

7. Correct the commutator flashcard.
Location: Section 2 Key Terms.
Current wording says a commutator in motors and generators reverses current to maintain consistent rotation.
Fix: A simple DC motor's split-ring commutator reverses coil current each half-turn to preserve torque direction. In a DC generator, it switches connections to produce unidirectional, pulsating external output. Contrast with slip rings for the simple AC generator. The newer AC/DC diagram already supports this distinction.

8. Complete the diagrams in the five older Process blocks.
Missing: Section 1 Introduction to Electromagnetic Induction (4 steps); Section 2 Electric Motors (5), How a Generator Produces Electricity (5), Dynamic Microphones (4); Section 3 Optimizing an Electromagnetic Crane (6).
Do not merely decorate them. Show the changing state in each step: current arrows, magnetic-field direction, force/motion arrows, flux or meter response, and closed/open circuit as relevant. Replace the crane process with the generator data walkthrough above rather than illustrating redundant crane content.
The two existing calculation walkthroughs have four illustrated steps each, correct results, and native LaTeX. Keep calculations in text, not baked into replacement images, and do not repeat step headings in images.

9. Strengthen flux and Lenz's-law scaffolding.
Location: Section 1 definitions, generic Process, orientation panel, sorting and Lenz introduction.
Use changing magnetic flux through the circuit rather than any change in magnetic environment or any motion. Include a still magnet, a moving magnet, and a translating coil in a uniform field with unchanged orientation/area as a counterexample.
Define the coil normal and theta visually before using Phi = BA cos(theta). State a fixed normal/sign convention, or explicitly use flux magnitude in entering/leaving sorting statements. Replace nature resists sudden changes with induced current opposes the change in flux. In the numerical example, state into the page and viewing direction in the initial givens, not only later.

10. Improve magnetic braking explanation and diagram.
Location: Section 3 flashcard/tab and diagram.
The diagram is legible and shows opposing motion/force, but its eddy-current loops are circles without directional arrows and the relevant field-through-plate region is unclear. Add field dots/crosses, current directions consistent with entering/leaving the localized field, and a thermal-energy destination.
Explain that ordinary eddy-current drag diminishes as speed falls and cannot hold a stationary vehicle; additional stopping/holding equipment is needed. Do not describe reduced energy loss as the goal of a dissipative brake without explaining that removing kinetic energy is its purpose. Distinguish this from regenerative braking if introduced.

11. Add independent application questions after the worked examples.
Current native MCs are all qualitative. Several have very weak distractors (random current direction, gravity causing microphone output, magnetic fields cooling a motor).
Keep the two worked examples, then add targeted checks using calculations, controlled comparisons, waveform interpretation, and data selection. The generator game works in the first tested round (1200 rpm -> 9.5 V, cost 48 under budget 70), but its voltage/cost values are not explained before the choice. Label them as model results, show a prediction/data comparison, and require evidence rather than only finding a passing setting.

12. Reduce repeated prose and improve sequence.
Both numerical Process blocks appear after closing/next-lesson transition text. Put each example before practice and the true conclusion. Condense lengthy introductions, repeated flashcard definitions, summary quotations and generic congratulations. Combine the overlapping Electrical Resistance/Heat Production explanation while retaining distinct mechanism and energy-accounting ideas. Replace lost as friction with transferred to thermal energy through friction: friction is a mechanism, not an energy form. Change Battery Voltage (Input Energy) to Battery Voltage (Energy per Charge). Clarify peak output and held-constant conditions in generator panels; avoid treating higher output as automatically higher efficiency.
No major spelling pattern emerged. Content precision, structure, question design, and older image-free processes are more significant than proofreading errors.

13. Improve equation accessibility.
The native equations render visually, but their accessible descriptions repeat instructions rather than reading the actual calculation. For example, the whole-chain equation's description says to use the original input, without stating 90 divided by 200 equals 45 percent. Add concise spoken equivalents including values, units, and results. This is an accessibility content finding, not a completed screen-reader test.

SUGGESTED PRACTICE SET (PROPOSED, NOT ADDED)
A. Faraday: N=80, per-turn flux increases 0.003 Wb in 0.20 s. Average emf = 1.2 V. Options 0.015, 0.24, 1.2, 24 V; feedback should identify the missing N/time mistakes.
B. Current: two coils each have emf 2.0 V but total resistances 5 and 10 ohms. Currents 0.40 and 0.20 A. Ask why equal emf does not imply equal current.
C. Ideal rotating generator: at fixed N, B and A, doubling angular speed doubles peak emf and frequency. A voltage-time sketch should make both changes visible.
D. Whole-chain efficiency: generator 80%, motor 75%, initial 300 J -> 180 J useful and 60% overall. Use distractors based on adding efficiencies or using the wrong energy denominator.
E. Design evidence: goal at least 8 V, budget <=70 units. A: 7.5 V/40; B: 8.5 V/65; C: 10 V/90. B is the only design meeting both constraints. Require two measured facts in the explanation.
F. AC interpretation: a negative voltmeter reading indicates reversed polarity relative to the meter leads, not negative energy production. Show one complete cycle and ask which reading is peak magnitude.
G. Heating: regulated I=2 A; R decreases 3 to 2 ohms -> power decreases 12 to 8 W. State that this conclusion depends on fixed current.

NEW WORKED EXAMPLE MOST NEEDED
A native Process called Use generator data to choose and defend a design:
1. Identify the output goal and constraints (annotated generator + goal/budget symbols).
2. Plan one-variable trials (controls diagram and blank table).
3. Record and compare peak voltages over several rotations (meter/waveform diagram).
4. Reject designs violating a constraint (visual comparison table).
5. Support a final choice with two measurements and Faraday's law (energy-flow and evidence diagram).
If numerical calculations are included, put them in native LaTeX text. Do not reuse the assignment's exact answer values as the worked example.
