											95 heading Understanding Electric Field Direction: The Role of the Test Charge, Value: 2
												96 text Understanding Electric Field Direction: The Role of the Test Charge
											97 text The direction of an electric field is always defined by the force it would exert on a positive test charge. If you place a small, positive test charge near another charged object (the source charge), the direction the test charge moves shows the field’s direction. It’s important to distinguish between the source charge, which creates the field, and the test charge, which is used to detect the field’s effects. This convention helps us consistently describe and draw electric fields.

											195 heading What Makes an Electric Field Strong or Weak?, Value: 2
												196 text What Makes an Electric Field Strong or Weak?
											197 text Electric field strength tells us how powerful the field is at a specific location. The closer you are to the source charge, the stronger the field becomes. As you move farther away, the field’s strength decreases rapidly. In diagrams, field strength is often shown by how close together the field lines are: the denser the lines, the stronger the field at that spot.

											251 text In real-life situations, electric fields often come from more than one charge. When this happens, the fields from each charge combine to create a net electric field.
