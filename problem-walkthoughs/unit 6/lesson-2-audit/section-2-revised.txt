Key Terms: Electric Potential and Related Concepts

Keep potential (energy per charge), potential energy, and work distinct.

Electric potential energy per unit charge at a position relative to a chosen zero: V = U/q. Unit: volt (V), equal to joule per coulomb (J/C).

The final potential minus the initial potential: ΔV = Vfinal − Vinitial. The electric field points toward decreasing potential.

Electric Potential Energy
Click to flip

Energy of a charge–source system due to charge positions. For a charge q moved through ΔV, ΔU = qΔV. Its sign depends on both q and ΔV.

Work
Click to flip

Energy transferred by a force during displacement. Electric-field work is WE = −ΔU. For constant-speed motion with only electric and external forces, Wext = ΔU.

Connecting Electric Fields and Potential: Why Direction Matters

The electric field points toward lower electric potential. A positive charge experiences force along the field; a negative charge experiences force opposite it. These forces change velocity. A charge already moving can temporarily travel against its electric force.

Worked example: Connect voltage, field, and work

Parallel plates are 0.040 m apart. The left plate is at 120 V and the right plate at 0 V. A +2.0 μC charge moves from right to left at constant speed. Neglect edge effects and other forces.

START
2 of 6
Step 1
Determine field direction and size

Electric field points from higher to lower potential, so it points right. Use the voltage difference magnitude divided by plate separation for a uniform field.

3 of 6
Step 2
Find the electric force

The positive charge feels a rightward electric force. To move it left at constant speed, an external agent supplies an equal leftward force.

4 of 6
Step 3
Calculate potential-energy change

Use final minus initial potential. The positive charge moves to higher potential and gains electric potential energy.

5 of 6
Step 4
Distinguish the two kinds of work

Electric work is negative because electric force opposes displacement. At constant speed, the external agent supplies the positive energy increase.

6 of 6
Check the complete result

The external agent supplies 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ J, stored as electric potential energy. The electric field does the same magnitude of negative work.

Energy in Electric Fields: Potential Energy and Work

Calculate ΔV first, then use ΔU = qΔV. Electric-field work is WE = −ΔU. If only the field does work, ΔK = WE. If an external force keeps the charge at constant speed, ΔK = 0 and Wext = ΔU.

Worked example: Negative charge, energy, and work

A −2.0 μC charge moves from the right plate at 20 V to the left plate at 80 V. An external force keeps its speed constant. Only the electric and external forces do work.

START
2 of 6
Step 1
Find the signed potential change

Use final minus initial potential. The charge moves left, toward higher potential; the electric field points right.

3 of 6
Step 2
Keep the charge sign

Convert microcoulombs to coulombs without dropping the negative sign. The electric force points left, opposite the field and along the displacement.

4 of 6
Step 3
Calculate the energy change

Multiply the signed charge by the signed voltage change. Although potential increases, this negative charge loses potential energy.

5 of 6
Step 4
Separate electric and external work

The electric force does positive work. To keep speed constant, the external force points right and does equal negative work. The two works sum to zero.

6 of 6
Check the complete result

The system loses 120 μJ of electric potential energy. The external agent removes that energy, so kinetic energy stays constant. If the external force were absent, electric work would increase kinetic energy instead.
