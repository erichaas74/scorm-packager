											96 heading How Do Charges Move and Settle in Materials?, Value: 2
												97 text How Do Charges Move and Settle in Materials?
											98 text The way charges behave in a material depends on its atomic structure. In conductors, free electrons can move throughout the material, allowing charges to spread out and reach a balanced state called electrostatic equilibrium. In insulators, electrons are tightly bound to their atoms, so charges stay where they are placed and do not move easily. This difference explains why metals conduct electricity and plastics do not.

												135 text How Do Objects Become Electrically Charged?
											136 text Objects can become electrically charged in two main ways: by contact or by induction. Charging by contact involves touching two objects together, allowing electrons to transfer directly. Charging by induction happens when a charged object is brought near a neutral one, causing electrons to rearrange without direct contact. Grounding is a key part of these processes, as it lets excess charge flow safely to or from the Earth.

												172 text How Can Neutral Objects Be Attracted to Charged Ones?
											173 text Even objects with no net charge can be attracted to charged objects through a process called polarization. In polarization, the charges within a neutral object rearrange slightly—positive and negative charges shift in opposite directions—creating a temporary attraction. This effect is especially noticeable in insulators, where the charges can't move freely but can still shift within molecules.
