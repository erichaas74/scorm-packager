HIGHER THAN YOU STARTED — Unit 4 Lesson 4

Open index.html to play. The sibling stored-energy-workshop-rise.zip contains this one activity, with index.html at the ZIP root.

Student sequence
1. Predict and test the rough ramp. The default 20% loss gives a return height of 0.80 m from a 1.00 m start.
2. Insert a mechanism. A domino/string alone cannot lift the captured ball.
3. Adjust an energy source to reach at least 1.25 m.
4. Reach the target using a second form of stored energy. Spring and rubber band count as the same elastic form.

Suggested investigations
- Disconnect the release. Explain why the energy remains stored.
- Set the weight to 0.12 kg and increase its drop. Explain why more available energy does not overcome insufficient driving force.
- Use a 0.18 kg counterweight with a 1.30 m drop. It lifts the cup and ball 1.30 m.
- Compare 0.20 m and 0.23 m spring compression. Their predicted lifts are 1.00 m and 1.3225 m.
- Calculate the minimum battery energy for the target: (0.10 + 0.02) × 10 × 1.25 / 0.60 = 2.50 J.
- Find a successful run with unused energy. Explain why the travel stop matters.

Model
All assumptions appear in the expandable model notes. This is a governed lift: the counterweight must overcome the load plus resistance, and a governor/brake dissipates excess energy. All powered trials capture the ball first, dissipating its initial energy, before lifting it slowly. The pulley redirects a pull and does not create energy. Kinetic energy in the slow lift is neglected. The test notebook resets when the page reloads.

Verification
Run test-model.cjs with Node to verify energy accounting and limiting cases. Browser checks covered baseline unlocking, successful counterweight and spring trials, battery shortfall, prediction feedback, challenge completion, reset, and playback controls.
