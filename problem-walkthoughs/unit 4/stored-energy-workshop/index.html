How this model accounts for energy

The ball has a mass of 0.10 kg. Use g = 10 N/kg. Its initial gravitational potential energy is 1.00 J relative to the bottom of the ramp. The rough patch converts the selected share into thermal energy; the return ramp is ideal. The ramp-only run pauses at its turning point.

With a lift inserted, the ball stops in a 0.02 kg cup. Its remaining kinetic energy is transferred into thermal energy and sound during capture. The extra source then raises BOTH the ball and cup slowly. A brake holds the cup; the lift is modeled as quasi-static, so its kinetic energy is neglected. The source’s setup work is included as stored energy.

Spring drive: E = ½kx², k = 80 N/m, with 75% of released energy reaching the load. Rubber-band drive: an ideal linear elastic model with k = 40 N/m and 70% transfer. A real rubber band is not perfectly linear. The drives represent elastic mechanisms coupled through gearing to a lifting drum, not a bare spring extending the entire lifting distance. Battery drive: 60% of released chemical energy reaches the load.

The fixed-pulley counterweight must overcome the 0.12 kg load and an additional 0.10 N resistance. A governor controls speed; surplus energy becomes thermal energy in friction and braking. Cup rise equals counterweight drop. Stored energy that is not released remains in the source. Maximum lift travel is 1.40 m.

The drawings and timing are simplified. Heights and energy budgets follow the stated model, not measurements from a real machine. Thermal energy is still energy; it has not disappeared.
