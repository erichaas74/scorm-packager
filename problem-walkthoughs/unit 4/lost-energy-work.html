The work-energy theorem with friction

Applied Work Is Not Net Work

Step 1 · Name the work

A 100 N push acts over 5 m. Does 100 N × 5 m automatically equal the net work?

Ask which force the formula uses. Wnet = Fnet × d, not Fapplied × d when friction is present.

Calculate Net Work

The push does +500 J of work, while friction does −200 J. What is the net work?

Fnet = 100 N − 40 N = 60 N. Use Wnet = Fnet × d, or add the signed work done by both forces.

Friction Does Negative Work

The push stops, so friction is now the only horizontal force. The crate starts with 300 J of kinetic energy.

As friction does negative net work, the crate's kinetic energy falls from 300 J to 0 J.
